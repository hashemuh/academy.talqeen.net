What We Cover

Our Islamic Studies programme covers all the essential topics a Muslim needs — for children, adults, and families seeking structured Islamic knowledge.

Aqeedah

The pillars of Islamic belief — Allah, His Angels, Books, Prophets, the Last Day, and Divine Decree. Presented clearly for every age.

Fiqh — Islamic Rulings

Prayer, purification, fasting, charity, and everyday fiqh — practical knowledge every Muslim needs from childhood.

Tafseer

The meaning, context, and wisdom behind Quranic verses — deepening the student's relationship with Allah's words.

Seerah — Life of the Prophet ﷺ

The story of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ — his character, his mission, and his legacy — told in a way that builds love for the Sunnah.

Hadith Studies

Selected collections of authentic hadith with explanation and application — including Arba'een An-Nawawi, Riyadh us-Saliheen, and more.

Islamic Manners & Character

Adab, daily Adhkar, and the manners of a Muslim in family life, social interaction, and worship.

Syllabus and Learning Stages

This sequence is adapted to the assessed starting level; familiar material can be reviewed and difficult skills revisited.

1

Belief and learning foundations

Study the pillars of belief and Islam with vocabulary and examples suited to the learner's level.

2

Practical worship

Learn the relevant foundations of purification, prayer, fasting and other worship, with clear explanations and opportunities for questions.

3

Seerah, hadith and Quranic meaning

Explore selected prophetic events, reliably referenced hadith and guided explanations of suitable Quranic passages.

4

Manners and thoughtful application

Connect learning with honesty, respect, responsibilities and daily situations, while revisiting misunderstood ideas.

What Happens in a Lesson

  1. Review a key idea or question from the previous lesson.
  2. Study a short text, explanation or age-appropriate account.
  3. Discuss vocabulary, context and the supporting reference.
  4. Work through a practical example or explain the idea in your own words.
  5. Agree on a small reading, recall or reflection task.

Assessment and Feedback

Checks include explaining concepts in your own words, identifying reliable references, recalling key points and applying suitable examples. Personal legal questions or disputed matters may require further qualified guidance beyond an introductory lesson.

Materials and Setup

  • Teacher-selected age-appropriate texts and lesson notes.
  • Quran passages and hadith with identifiable references.
  • A notebook for key terms, questions and practical examples.
  • A suitable translation when Arabic knowledge is still developing.

Confirm the required edition and any material costs with the team before purchasing resources.

Realistic Progression

Subject knowledge grows through repeated study and application. Introductory lessons are not a scholarly qualification, and the syllabus should be adjusted to the learner's age, background and questions. Confirm the approach to fiqh differences and advanced topics with the teacher.

Possible next step: Arabic foundations for further textual study. The teacher can help you choose when the foundations are ready.
