A Realistic Hifz Programme

Memorising the Quran is one of the greatest acts a Muslim can undertake. Our structured approach makes it achievable — even for adults with busy lives.

New Sabak

Each lesson introduces new verses at a pace matched to your ability — enough to memorise solidly, not so much that it overwhelms.

Recent Revision

The verses you memorised in the past week are reviewed every session to lock them in before moving forward.

Old Revision

Previously memorized material is reviewed systematically so weak passages can be identified and strengthened. Retention needs continuing practice.

Tajweed Integrated

Memorisation without Tajweed is incomplete. Your teacher ensures every verse is memorised with correct pronunciation and recitation rules.

For Children and Adults

Children and adults can benefit from a plan matched to reading skill, attention, responsibilities and revision needs. Progress varies between learners.

Completion Certificate

Students who complete their memorisation receive a certificate of completion — a record of one of the greatest achievements in a Muslim's life.

Syllabus and Learning Stages

This sequence is adapted to the assessed starting level; familiar material can be reviewed and difficult skills revisited.

1

Placement and revision baseline

Recite familiar material, identify weak sections and agree on a realistic starting portion and review routine.

2

Accurate new memorization

Prepare a checked passage, divide it into manageable units and connect them before reciting from memory.

3

Recent and older revision

Maintain separate review tasks for newly learned passages and material memorized earlier.

4

Retention checks and adjustment

Recite without looking, revisit recurring errors and adjust new work when revision needs more attention.

What Happens in a Lesson

  1. Review the assigned recent or older material.
  2. Recite prepared new learning from memory.
  3. Correct reading and recall errors before further repetition.
  4. Connect passages and practise a suitable recall check.
  5. Agree on new work and revision targets for the next session.

Assessment and Feedback

Progress is assessed through oral recitation, recall without looking and the reliability of older material. The teacher may reduce new work or assign revision-only periods when retention needs support.

Materials and Setup

  • One clear Mushaf used consistently for reading and preparation.
  • A revision chart separating new work, recent review and older review.
  • An error log with the passages that need another check.
  • Teacher-selected recordings matching the reading being studied.

Confirm the required edition and any material costs with the team before purchasing resources.

Realistic Progression

There is no single completion timetable for every learner. Verse length, reading skill, study time and retention vary. A slower plan with dependable revision is preferable to increasing page targets while older material weakens.

Possible next step: Tajweed support for accurate memorization. The teacher can help you choose when the foundations are ready.
