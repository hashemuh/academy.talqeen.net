How to Make Time for Quran Study as an Adult: 10 Practical Tips

Talqeen Academy · Updated 5 October 2026, 08:35:26 (UTC+03:00)

Work, higher education and household responsibilities can make Quran study difficult to organise. The answer is often a small, repeatable routine rather than waiting for a completely free day. Decide what you want to practise, reserve a manageable period and return to it consistently.

The source recommends using mornings, suitable office breaks, commute listening and flexible adult lessons. These can help, but each needs to fit your actual responsibilities and attention. The ten approaches below connect brief daily practice with opportunities for deeper weekly study.

10 Ways to Make Room for Quran Study

1. Choose a fixed, realistic daily period

Select a time that is usually available: early morning, a lunch break or before bed may work. Begin with around ten to fifteen minutes if that is realistic, and give the period a specific purpose, such as reading, revision or a small meaning-study task.

Use an alarm or reminder, and record what you completed. A notebook can hold questions, useful explanations and points to review with a teacher. Increase the time only when the routine is comfortable; consistency is more useful than repeatedly missing an ambitious plan.

2. Consider a session after Fajr

A quiet period after Fajr may suit people who are alert then and do not immediately need to leave for work. Keep your Mushaf or selected resource ready so the session can begin without searching for materials. Even a brief session can include careful reading and reflection.

The source cites Al-Isra 17:78: “Establish prayer at the decline of the sun until the darkness of the night and [recite] the Qur’an at dawn. Indeed, the recitation of the Qur’an at dawn is ever witnessed.” This concerns prayer and dawn recitation; it does not establish a universal scientific peak for memory.

The source calls Fajr the best memorisation time and says many Hifz learners use it. Treat it as a practical suggestion rather than a requirement. If another time lets you practise more attentively and reliably, plan around that.

3. Use an appropriate office break

The source describes thirty- to sixty-minute breaks and suggests reserving ten to fifteen minutes for reading or listening. Break arrangements differ, so use a period that is actually available and does not conflict with your work responsibilities.

A pocket Mushaf or a suitable app can make access easier. A quiet corner, your desk where appropriate, or headphones for a recording may help. You do not need to finish a chapter: read a few ayahs carefully or reflect on a familiar passage when active reading is impractical.

4. Reallocate some optional screen time

Notice how much time you spend scrolling and consider replacing fifteen to twenty minutes with Quran reading or reflection. The aim is a practical change in your own routine, not a judgement about every use of social media.

Tools such as Screen Time or Digital Wellbeing may help you see usage and set limits where available. If you continue using social platforms, choose educational accounts thoughtfully and check the sources behind their explanations. A short post is not a substitute for a structured lesson.

5. Use digital resources for quick access

The source names four resources, with different roles in study:

  • Quran.com: Arabic text, translations, Tafsir and recordings can support reading and understanding.
  • Quran Companion: the source describes it as a way to track progress and set goals; check the current product and available features.
  • iQuran: its publisher’s listing describes verse-level and word-by-word translation features. Check your platform and version.
  • Tarteel: its developer provides memorisation support and mistake-detection features under relevant plans. It is a supplementary tool, not a replacement for a teacher’s recitation assessment.

Preview what you need, confirm offline access if relevant and avoid collecting more apps than you actually use. See Quran.com, iQuran’s publisher listing and Tarteel’s feature guidance. For guided learning, explore Talqeen Academy’s courses.

6. Give a small passage attentive study

Quality includes both accurate recitation and appropriate understanding. You might read a ruku or smaller section, note a question and return to it during the week. Pause to absorb the explanation rather than treating completed pages as the only achievement.

Discuss a difficult point with a knowledgeable teacher. A family discussion can also support learning when participants distinguish an explanation from a personal reflection. Keep the task manageable so study remains regular.

7. Listen during suitable journeys

A bus or train journey can provide time for a downloaded recitation or Tafsir lesson. The source also mentions car journeys, YouTube and Quran apps. Choose reliable recordings and check whether the service permits the offline access you want.

Ten to fifteen minutes on several journeys adds meaningful listening time. Keep travel attention and responsibilities first, and avoid operating a device while driving. Listening supports familiarity and understanding, but active reading and teacher feedback still need their own place.

8. Include family study where possible

Set aside around ten to fifteen minutes for shared reading, an explanation or a review activity if the family can manage it. Take turns reciting, discuss a suitable meaning or introduce a short surah. A friendly question about a Quranic story can encourage participation.

If weekday schedules do not overlap, choose a weekend session instead. Adjust the task for different ages and abilities, and avoid turning family time into a comparison. Continue with explaining Islam to children for age-appropriate discussion.

9. Review a manageable short surah

The source suggests Al-Fil (105), Al-Asr (103), Al-Ikhlas (112) and Al-Falaq (113), all in Juz Amma. Their short length can make them practical study portions, but each still needs accurate pronunciation and suitable explanation.

  • Choose one passage and have the reading checked.
  • Read or review it regularly.
  • Study a suitable meaning rather than guessing.
  • Use securely learned material in Salah according to appropriate prayer practice.
  • Add more only when the routine and recall remain reliable.

An adult beginner may start with these passages or another teacher-selected route. See planning a learning sequence.

10. Reserve a longer weekend session

The source suggests thirty to sixty minutes for deeper work when a weekend offers more time. Choose a clear goal: studying part of a Tafsir lesson, reviewing memorised material or practising a Tajweed point.

Prepare a quiet space and use the notebook to connect this session with the week’s questions. A longer weekly period supplements the small daily habit; it does not have to replace it. Online lessons may help organise this work when their schedule fits.

Build a Routine That Fits Adult Life

Small periods of attentive learning can create a continuing connection with the Quran. Plan active practice, use listening appropriately and review what you have already studied. A missed day is a reason to return to the routine, not abandon it.

The source promotes experienced Egyptian teachers and personalised support for learners in the UK, USA, Canada, Australia and Europe. Assess the actual teacher and programme rather than assuming promotional descriptions establish quality. Continue with balancing Hifz with a busy schedule to develop a revision-focused plan.
