How to Convert to Islam: Shahada, Belief and a Complete Beginner Plan

Talqeen Academy · Updated 5 October 2026, 08:35:26 (UTC+03:00)

Conversion to Islam centres on sincere acceptance of Allah alone as worthy of worship and Muhammad ﷺ as His Messenger. Express that belief through the Shahada with understanding. You can do so at home or with support; a certificate, fee or official ceremony is not a religious prerequisite.

The declaration can be brief, while the study that leads to it and the learning afterwards take different amounts of time for different people. This guide explains the testimony, its meaning, the first practical duties and questions that often arise about names, family and community.

The Shahada and its meaning

أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلٰهَ إِلَّا ٱللَّٰهُ وَأَشْهَدُ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ ٱللَّٰه

Transliteration: Ash-hadu an lā ilāha illā Allāh, wa ash-hadu anna Muḥammadan rasūlu Allāh.

Meaning: I bear witness that there is no god worthy of worship except Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah.

The two partsExplanation
Allah aloneReject worship directed to anything other than Allah and affirm His oneness.
The MessengerAccept the message and guidance of Muhammad ﷺ.
SincerityThe tongue expresses a belief genuinely accepted, rather than a phrase repeated without meaning.

Reports concerning sincere testimony and the Hereafter encourage belief with truthfulness. Do not interpret them as permission to ignore the rest of the religion or as a personal certificate deciding anyone's ultimate judgement.

Seven themes used to explain sincere testimony

ThemeWhat it explains
Knowledge — al-ilmUnderstand what the testimony affirms and rejects.
Certainty — al-yaqinAccept the belief sincerely rather than treating the testimony as a performance; Quran 49:15 discusses faithful conviction.
Sincerity — al-ikhlasDirect worship to Allah, rather than merely seek social advantage.
Truthfulness — al-sidqLet the spoken declaration reflect what is genuinely believed.
Love — al-mahabbahValue Allah, His Messenger and the guidance accepted.
Submission — al-inqiyadAccept responsibility to follow the guidance and make a sincere effort.
Acceptance — al-qabulDo not knowingly reject the meaning of the testimony while claiming it.

These are a common scholarly explanation of the testimony's inward requirements, associated in the source with classical scholarship including Ibn al-Qayyim. They are not seven forms to submit or an exam every newcomer must pass. Only Allah knows the heart; a checklist should not be advertised as a mechanical guarantee of acceptance.

Eight practical steps for a new Muslim

1. Understand tawhid

Study the belief that Allah has no partners or equals and is the unique Creator. Surah Al-Ikhlas, 112:1–4, provides a concise introduction. You need genuine acceptance of the foundation, not complete expertise in theology before beginning.

2. Learn the testimony

Read or memorise its wording and ask about any phrase you do not understand. If Arabic is difficult, state the meaning in your own language and practise the Arabic gradually. Fluency is not an entry requirement.

3. State it sincerely

Choose a suitable moment and say both testimonies with conviction. You may ask a knowledgeable person to guide you, but the declaration belongs to your own belief, not to the authority of the person listening.

A new beginningExplanation
ForgivenessSahih Muslim 121 teaches that Islam wipes away what preceded it.
RightsDo not assume that debts, property claims or duties towards others disappear.

4. Learn purification

Ghusl is discussed after conversion as obligatory by some scholars and recommended by others. It is not a precondition of the testimony. Learn the full method and any other relevant purification requirements instead of treating the bath as only a symbol.

5. Begin prayer

Learn Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha, including the proper local times, purification, movements and recitations. Seek guidance for what to do while you are still learning. The source's promise that a newcomer will not be judged harshly should not become an invented blanket exemption.

6. Find suitable community support

A local mosque, mentor or moderated online group can provide help. The quality of support varies, so ask specific questions. Online contact may be useful, but it is not automatically equivalent to every practical benefit of a nearby community.

7. Study the foundations

Learn the five pillars of practice and six pillars of faith, along with everyday conduct. Keep the sequence manageable and return to topics that remain unclear.

8. Consider structured instruction

A beginner course can organise belief, prayer, Quran and daily life. Ask about teacher knowledge, language, format and pace. A paid course is one option for support, not a requirement for conversion.

Benefits and expectations

  • A framework for purpose and worship.
  • Hope in forgiveness and a fresh beginning.
  • Direct supplication to Allah.
  • Guidance for moral choices and daily conduct.
  • Connection with Muslims across cultures.
  • Regular worship through the five prayers.
  • Belief in the Hereafter and hope in Allah's mercy.
  • Study of the Quran and the Prophet's guidance.

New Muslims may describe peace or clarity, but emotional experiences vary. Becoming Muslim does not guarantee a life without hardship, and no academy can promise every outcome. The source's undated population total of 1.9 billion should not be treated as a current count.

The source makes broad claims about textual preservation and modern embryology, cosmology and oceanography. Muslims believe the Quran is preserved revelation, and it invites reflection on creation. Specific manuscript-history and scientific claims still require careful evidence and interpretation. Do not describe every modern theory as an exact prediction impossible for anyone earlier to know.

People may find passages about creation meaningful, including readers with scientific backgrounds. That does not establish how often such passages cause conversion or replace the need to understand the Quran's religious message in context.

Home, mosque or online?

A sincere testimony can be expressed privately at home, with Muslim friends, at a mosque or during a remote conversation. The setting supplies support or documentation, not a different category of religious belief. Saying it online does not require a special technology-based doctrine.

Witnesses can provide help, recognition and a connection with the community. The source describes two witnesses as encouraged; do not make that a universal condition for entry or confuse conversion with other legal procedures that have their own witness rules.

Choosing a settingExplanation
PrivateSuitable where a public announcement is unnecessary or difficult.
MosqueMay offer local support and appropriate documentation.
OnlineCan provide explanation and contact when distance or privacy matters.

If disclosure creates danger, seek suitable confidential support and decide carefully how to share the news. A witness or provider should not record or publish a declaration without your agreement.

WitnessingAgree who will be present.

Forgiveness, responsibilities and repentance

The source recounts Amr ibn al-As asking about forgiveness before his pledge. Sahih Muslim 121 contains that exchange and the teaching that Islam wipes away what came before.

Reading the reportExplanation
HopeA complicated past does not close the door to sincere acceptance.
ResponsibilityResolve matters involving others’ rights with knowledgeable guidance.
ContinuityAfter conversion, repentance and correction remain part of religious life.

A new Muslim joins the community, studies the Quran and Sunnah and undertakes worship. Hope in Paradise should be connected with sincere faith, righteous conduct and Allah's mercy, rather than stated as an automatic personal guarantee from a ceremonial declaration.

If you sin afterwards, repent sincerely: stop the wrongdoing, feel remorse and resolve not to return. Where another person's rights are involved, address them appropriately. Being imperfect is not an excuse to continue harm, nor a reason to despair.

Names and cultural identity

You ordinarily do not need an Arabic name. The source identifies names that imply service or worship of another deity, and names whose meaning conflicts with Islamic belief. Those require attention to actual meaning, not a blanket rule against every name associated with another culture or faith.

A chosen Muslim name can be personally meaningful, but keeping a suitable birth name does not make a declaration less valid. Ask about a specific name if uncertain rather than changing it solely because someone says it does not sound Muslim.

A first-thirty-days learning map

PeriodPractical focus
Day 1Learn relevant purification, make dua and begin prayer guidance. Rest and reflection can accompany these duties.
Days 2–7Continue wudu and Salah learning, practise Al-Fatihah and receive correction. Do not postpone every prayer until the week ends.
Week 2Strengthen the five-prayer routine, read a suitable translation and consider local support.
Week 3Arrange dependable instruction and contact considerate learners or a mentor.
Week 4Continue studying the pillars, prepare for Ramadan if relevant and set manageable goals for the next three to six months.

This is a planning example, not a religious timetable that delays essential learning. Topics can overlap, and someone embracing Islam during Ramadan needs guidance for the current month rather than waiting for the next year.

BeliefUnderstand the testimony and the articles of faith.
WorshipLearn purification, prayer and duties that presently apply.
QuranCombine reading, pronunciation and meaning.

Conversion numbers and projections

The source gives US conversions of 20,000–30,000 annually, UK conversions around 5,000 with over 60% women, thousands in Australia and Canada, and about 7,000 in France. None is accompanied here by a clearly dated underlying method that establishes a current annual count. Preserve the figures as source claims, not confirmed present-day facts.

Its reference to Islam becoming the largest religion by 2075 is a projection claim, not a guaranteed event. Pew's 2015 projection report now notes that its 2010 baseline estimates were subsequently updated. Population models depend on assumptions and should be labelled accordingly.

The claims that most Western converts are women and that online education has dramatically accelerated conversion also need defined evidence. Online resources certainly provide a route to study, but the supplied article does not establish a measured causal effect.

Annual countRequire a dated source, method and national scope.
Survey shareDo not confuse the percentage of Muslims raised elsewhere with annual conversions.
ProjectionState assumptions and distinguish future models from observed figures.

Women, family and practical questions

A woman can declare belief during menstruation and without male permission. She can learn, make dhikr and read explanations while obtaining guidance about prayer and later purification. Conversion is not restricted to Arabs or to any ethnicity.

Parents' permission is not a religious prerequisite for belief, and respectful conduct towards them remains important. A provider's safeguarding or consent policy for minors is a separate issue. No general article should assume that every country's administrative rules are identical.

Male circumcision is not a condition that must precede the testimony. The source's universal statement that it is always a later obligation needs qualification because legal assessments and individual circumstances differ. Obtain suitable guidance about the religious and health questions separately.
