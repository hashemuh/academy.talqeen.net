Easy Surahs to Memorize for Beginners: Where to Start

Talqeen Academy · Updated 5 October 2026, 00:26:04 (UTC+03:00)

Starting memorisation with short surahs builds confidence quickly and gives you passages to use in prayer. Which surahs to start with is a practical question, and the best answer is usually: begin with the shortest and the ones you already hear often.

The list below is a suggested order for beginners, from the briefest to somewhat longer. Your teacher may adjust it based on your level.

A suggested order

SurahVersesWhy it is a good starting point
Al-Fatiha7Recited in every unit of prayer, so it is practised constantly
Al-Ikhlas4Very short and often heard
Al-Falaq5Short, with a clear rhythm
An-Nas6Short, usually learned together with Al-Falaq
Al-Kawthar3The shortest surah
Al-Asr3Very short, easy to repeat
An-Nasr3Short and flowing
Al-Kafirun6Strong repetition pattern
Quraysh4Short and rhythmic
Al-Fil5Short narrative
Al-Ma'un7Slightly longer, still manageable
Al-Masad5Short with distinctive rhyme

Verse counts are the standard counts in the Mushaf. After these, learners often continue with surahs such as Ash-Sharh, Ad-Duha, Al-Qadr, At-Takathur and Al-Humazah.

Why start with these

  • They are short enough to learn in days, which builds the habit of finishing.
  • Many are recited regularly, so you hear them and use them.
  • Success early on makes longer surahs less intimidating.

A simple method for each surah

  1. Listen to a reliable reciter several times, following the text.
  2. Read it slowly with a teacher, correcting any mispronunciation.
  3. Learn one verse at a time, repeating it until it flows, then link it to the previous verse.
  4. Recite the whole surah without looking, then check.
  5. Revise the next day, in three days, and in a week.

The method is covered in more depth in how to stop forgetting Juz Amma.

Pronunciation before speed

Memorising a surah with an error means unlearning it later. Ask a teacher to hear each surah before you consider it done. If you are still learning to read, the beginner roadmap shows how reading and memorising fit together.
