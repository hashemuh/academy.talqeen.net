Learning Quran Surahs in Order: A Practical Memorisation Sequence

Talqeen Academy · Updated 5 October 2026, 08:35:26 (UTC+03:00)

A clear learning sequence can make Quran study easier to organise. Instead of choosing a new passage without a plan, agree with a teacher on the next target and how earlier material will be reviewed. Starting with short surahs in Juz Amma is one practical approach, not the only valid order.

The source recommends moving from An-Nas towards earlier surahs and then progressing to longer passages. This reverse learning order differs from the Quran’s printed surah order. Either can be used for study when the plan suits the learner and preserves accurate reading and revision.

8 Steps for a Structured Surah-Learning Plan

1. Learn the Arabic letters and their articulation

The source calls the letters “Huruful Ijahiyat”; the usual teaching term is huruf al-hija, or alphabet letters. Learn their shapes and sounds, then practise them within words. The source’s table gives six examples rather than the whole alphabet and contains several articulation errors. The corrected introductory table follows.

LetterIntroductory articulation description
أ / ء — hamzahThe deepest part of the throat; distinguish hamzah from a long-vowel alif.
هـ — haThe deepest part of the throat, not the middle.
ع — aynThe middle of the throat.
غ — ghaynThe upper part of the throat, nearest the mouth.
ق — qafThe rear of the tongue against the corresponding upper area.
ك — kafA nearby tongue articulation slightly farther forward than qaf.

A long-vowel alif is not simply the hamzah shown on an alif carrier. Use a teacher’s spoken examples to understand these distinctions. The Learn Tajweed reference can support the lesson, but a table cannot hear your pronunciation.

2. Recognise joined letters in Quranic words

Practise how letters appear at the beginning, middle and end of words, and notice which letters do not connect to the following letter. A connected word is not necessarily one uninterrupted written shape.

The source uses الرحمن and الصلاة as familiar examples. Examine their actual printed forms with the teacher instead of assuming every neighbouring letter joins. Read the words accurately and recognise the same letter patterns elsewhere.

Joined-letter recognition supports fluency, but smooth reading still needs correct vowels and articulation. Continue with reading the Quran in Arabic for beginners.

3. Apply simple Tajweed examples correctly

The source introduces ghunna, qalqalah and madd. Keep these three topics, but correct the examples that would otherwise teach an error.

  • Ghunna: إِنَّ contains a doubled nun with a nasal sound.
  • Qalqalah: the source points to qaf in قُلْ, but that qaf has a vowel. Qalqalah applies to its relevant letters when sakin; قَدْ provides an example with a sakin dal.
  • Madd: in الرَّحِيم, the long “ee” involves ya after kasrah, not the initial alif described in the source. Learn its normal length and any stopping-related change with the teacher.

Practise the actual sound rather than a rule name alone. Learn the next rule when it appears in the current passage, keeping earlier corrections in review.

4. Consider beginning with Juz Amma

Juz Amma is the thirtieth juz and contains many short surahs, including An-Nas and Al-Falaq. Familiar passages used in prayer may offer manageable early targets. Children can work through suitable portions with a teacher who adapts the amount.

Starting here is a useful option, not an essential requirement for every learner. Short surahs still need careful reading, and prior familiarity should be checked for accuracy. Explore Talqeen Academy’s learning options to discuss a starting plan.

5. Move from An-Nas to Al-Falaq and earlier surahs if appropriate

The source proposes learning An-Nas, then Al-Falaq, Al-Ikhlas and earlier surahs within Juz Amma. This creates a visible sequence and can cover passages commonly recited in prayer.

Surah length and difficulty do not increase perfectly one by one in reverse order. Let the teacher adjust the portion and pace rather than assuming each next surah is automatically harder or longer. Check a learned surah before adding another.

6. Introduce longer surahs gradually

The source names Al-Mulk (67), Yaseen (36), Al-Baqarah (2) and Al-Imran (3) as possible later targets. These are examples, not a required progression or a claim that one sequence is best for everyone. Their place in the plan depends on your goal and available revision time.

Divide longer passages into suitable portions, learn the connections and keep earlier material active. This gradual approach lets you build a larger review routine without making length itself a reason to rush.

7. Continue through earlier juz with accurate labels

A plan may continue from Juz 30 to Juz 29, then 28 and 27. Juz 29 begins with Al-Mulk and is commonly associated with “Tabarak.” Juz 28 begins with قَدْ سَمِعَ in Al-Mujadilah 58:1, while Juz 27 begins with قَالَ فَمَا خَطْبُكُمْ at Adh-Dhariyat 51:31.

The source labels Juz 28 “Qad Aflaha” and Juz 27 “Wa Ma Ubrioo.” Those are not the correct opening labels for these two juz and are corrected here. Use the actual juz number and Mushaf boundaries when planning.

Earlier juz are not uniformly short or easy. Expect the material and review load to change, and discuss meanings alongside memorisation where suitable.

8. Repeat and revise before moving forward

Return to the previous surah, test recall and correct uncertainty before adding the next. Schedule daily or weekly review according to the amount you have memorised, keeping both recent and older passages in the cycle.

The source cites Sahih al-Bukhari 6464: “Do good deeds properly, sincerely and moderately and know that your deeds will not make you enter Paradise, and that the most beloved deed to Allah is the most regular and constant even if it were little.” Its guidance on regularity can encourage steady practice; it does not prescribe a particular Hifz order or repetition count.

In What Order Should You Learn the Quran?

One route starts with short surahs in Juz Amma, continues into Juz Tabarak and then works through earlier sections. Another follows the printed order or a teacher-selected sequence. Choose a plan that reflects your reading ability, goals and revision capacity.

The source presents the shorter-surah route as its tutors’ strategy. It can provide achievable milestones, but learners need not all follow it. For help with early decisions, read where to start memorising the Quran.

Use Structure Without Losing Flexibility

A sequence helps you know what comes next, while revision keeps what came before reliable. Start with appropriate material, learn it accurately and adjust the pace when passages become more demanding. A completed surah should remain part of the learning plan.

The source concludes by offering tailored online tutor support. Assess how the actual teacher checks recall and explains corrections. Continue with the Quran memorisation guide to connect the sequence with a sustainable review routine.
