Reciting Shahada in Arabic and English: Meaning, Steps and Context

Talqeen Academy · Updated 5 October 2026, 08:35:26 (UTC+03:00)

The Shahada is the testimony of faith affirming Allah's exclusive right to worship and Muhammad's messengership. A declaration expresses sincere belief; understanding should accompany the words.

This guide provides the Arabic sequence and its English meaning, then distinguishes established contexts from unsupported claims in the source.

The Arabic Declaration

أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَأَشْهَدُ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ.

A common transliteration is “Ash-hadu an la ilaha illa Allah, wa ash-hadu anna Muhammadan rasulullah.” Learn it in manageable clauses with a reliable demonstration.

  1. Begin with ash-hadu an: I bear witness that.
  2. Continue la ilaha illa Allah: none deserves worship except Allah.
  3. Say wa ash-hadu anna: and I bear witness that.
  4. Complete Muhammadan rasulullah: Muhammad is Allah's messenger.

The source incorrectly instructed readers to say Allah immediately after ash-hadu, then supplied an incomplete sequence missing the second testimony. Do not learn that erroneous ordering as the full Shahada.

The English Meaning

  1. Say “I bear witness” or “I testify.”
  2. Affirm that none deserves worship.
  3. Complete that affirmation with “except Allah.”
  4. Add “and I bear witness.”
  5. Affirm that Muhammad is Allah's messenger.

A clear English declaration helps a beginner express and understand the belief. Arabic pronunciation can improve gradually; sincerity is not created by repeating an unfamiliar sentence quickly.

Its Place in Islamic Belief

Shahada concerns testimony or witnessing and is the first of Islam's Five Pillars. It affirms monotheism and Muhammad's final prophethood and guides a commitment to Quranic and prophetic teaching.

The source also called the declaration a prayer asking for protection. Its actual wording is testimony rather than a direct supplication. A person may make a separate dua for guidance and protection.

Meaning, Significance and Purpose

Meaning: affirm the two beliefs with understanding. Significance: recognise their foundational place in Islam. Purpose: express faith and seek to live according to its guidance.

Shared belief connects people across languages and cultures. That connection should be accompanied by respectful support and practical learning rather than pressure to master every subject at once.

Learning Support and Registration

The source's section called “how to recite” actually described course registration: visit its academy, locate a new-Muslim course, choose a trial and submit details. Those were booking steps, not parts of the testimony or requirements for conversion.

A teacher or community can explain the words, answer questions and provide ongoing companionship. The source promoted such a learning community; verify current arrangements through actual course information.

Faith, Remembrance and a New Beginning

A sincere declaration begins entry into Islam and can remain a reminder of belief during daily life. Seeking forgiveness and guidance involves sincerity and appropriate practice, not an automatic result from repeating words without their meaning.

When Is the Testimony Used?

Embracing Islam

The declaration expresses the person's belief. A witness or teacher may offer support, but purchasing a course is not a condition.

Prayer

The testimony appears in tashahhud. The source incorrectly said it is recited separately in every unit of Salah. Learn the appropriate prayer wording and position from a competent instructor.

Funerals and the End of Life

Remembrance may occur around bereavement, but repeating words at a funeral cannot manufacture the deceased person's faith. Funeral prayer has its own taught requirements.

The source's FAQ headed “before death” instead answered a question about journeys. Abu Dawud 3116 concerns the significance of last words affirming Allah's oneness. Treat someone near death with care and avoid coercion or pronouncing a judgement on someone unable to speak.

Marriage and Family Occasions

The source mentioned marriage and “dedicating children” as occasions for testimony. General remembrance is distinct from a mandatory new Shahada ceremony at every wedding or a required child-dedication rite; the article supplied no basis for those requirements.

Journeys and Important Undertakings

A believer may remember their faith and make dua while beginning a task or journey. That was the theme of the source's misplaced answer. Do not invent an obligatory Shahada formula for every undertaking.

Shahada in a Dream

The original described such a dream as necessarily a divine sign and a mystical confirmation of faith. That interpretation is not guaranteed. Muslim 2263a distinguishes different kinds of dreams, including thoughts from the person's own mind.

A dream may feel meaningful and encourage reflection, but it does not establish a legal requirement, replace a conscious declaration or prove someone's final spiritual standing. Avoid treating vivid language about divine presence as an established creed.

Continue with Understanding

The testimony's significance continues in worship, conduct and remembrance. Learn gradually and ask for reliable answers. See new-Muslim learning, guidance and support and supplication study.
