Importance of Reading the Quran: Virtues, Daily Habits and Reliable Evidence

Talqeen Academy · Updated 5 October 2026, 08:35:26 (UTC+03:00)

Reading the Quran is an act of worship and a way to return regularly to Islamic guidance. Recitation, understanding and action support one another: a person learns the words accurately, studies what they mean and responds through conduct. A daily portion can make that relationship more consistent.

This guide preserves the supplied article's fifteen themes and its questions about manners, timing, Tajweed, reward and health. It distinguishes sound references from unsourced claims so that encouragement does not rely on promises of immunity from illness, hardship or wrongdoing.

The Quran as guidance

Quran 17:9 describes guidance towards what is most upright and good news for believers who do righteous deeds. Quran 14:1 speaks of bringing people from darkness to light by their Lord's permission. Both connect the text with guidance rather than reading as an isolated performance.

Bukhari 5027 praises learning and teaching the Quran. That teaching includes accuracy and responsibility, not merely passing on attractive quotations without checking their references. Keep a regular reading habit and seek explanation for passages you do not understand.

1. Rewards and special-surah reports

The source begins with several reports about particular passages. Their wording and assessment should remain separate: a sound general virtue does not authenticate every numerical promise attached to a surah.

  • Al-Ikhlas ten times and a palace: this report has been assessed differently by scholars. It should not be presented as undisputed. This assessment explains weakness in a cited route; other discussions consider supporting evidence.
  • Al-Kafirun as a quarter of the Quran: the source combines this with Al-Ikhlas as a third. The quarter claim has disputed or weak routes and should not be treated as having the same evidential status as the authentic third-of-the-Quran report.
  • Al-Ikhlas as a third: the established virtue is not a licence to replace reading or learning every other part of the Quran. Three repetitions are not literally a recitation of all its words.
  • One hundred verses at night: check the exact report. Abu Dawud 1398 connects one hundred verses in night prayer with being recorded among the obedient; that is not the same wording as every statement about a full night's standing.
  • Al-Kahf on Friday: reports concerning light between Fridays should be cited with their actual source and grading rather than used as a promise of a visible physical phenomenon.

These distinctions allow a reader to value established virtues without inventing ritual requirements or a reward calculation from a combined list.

2. Honour for parents

Reports describe honour associated with a child learning and acting upon the Quran. The supplied article mentions a crown of light and extraordinary garments but calls the garments “two jewels.” Do not merge different narrations or change the reported objects within an attributed quotation.

Parents can arrange sound teaching, encourage revision and support good conduct. The relevant reports should be studied with their grading and conditions; Abu Dawud 1453 is one commonly cited reference. Merely booking lessons does not guarantee intercession for the entire family.

Ongoing beneficial knowledge and a righteous child's prayer are important themes in Islamic teaching. A child is not automatically a permanent reward mechanism regardless of conduct; honour the child's learning needs rather than treating Hifz as pressure to secure a parent's status.

3. Hope in Paradise

The source cites a report concerning Ayat al-Kursi after obligatory prayer and entry into Paradise. It is a known virtue discussed by scholars, but the supplied article gives no exact reference or assessment. Share it with a reliable source rather than treating every paraphrase as a verified quotation.

Its statement that three readings of Al-Ikhlas equal a whole-Quran reading needs the earlier distinction: a described equivalence in virtue is not identical to reading every verse or fulfilling an obligation involving another passage. Continue learning the whole text.

4. Healing and mercy

Quran 17:82 describes the Quran as healing and mercy for believers, and 10:57 speaks of healing for what is in the hearts. These teachings have a spiritual and religious meaning. They do not justify a universal clinical guarantee that recitation cures every physical or mental illness.

Aishah's report about the Prophet reciting the protective surahs and blowing over himself during illness appears in Sahih Muslim 2192. Religious supplication and ruqyah can be part of a believer's life without cancelling the need for medical assessment or treatment.

The source promises remedies for every known or unknown discomfort. That should not be used to tell a sick person that continued symptoms prove poor faith, or to delay care because a recitation has not yet produced recovery.

5. Love and companionship

The authentic teaching that a person is with those they love encourages love for Allah, His Messenger and righteous people. The source applies it to love of the Quran. That is a reflective theme, not evidence that an individual's final company can be decided from an online reading claim.

Love should show in learning, respect and action. It is more useful to ask how the Quran is shaping your conduct than to use a title such as “person of the Quran” as a guarantee of status.

6. Reward for reading letters

Tirmidhi 2910 describes a good deed for a letter of Allah's book, multiplied tenfold, and explains that alif, lam and mim are separate letters. This encourages recitation even when a person's portion is small.

The source calls the reward “infinite” and the simplest way to multiply rewards. Keep to the report's actual encouragement without constructing unsupported totals or ranking every act of worship. Sincerity and proper conduct remain important.

7. Intercession

Muslim 804a encourages reading the Quran and describes its intercession for its companions on the Day of Resurrection. This is a reason to maintain an enduring relationship with the text.

The source adds that even parents will not recognise their children at that moment. That dramatic detail should not be attached to this report without evidence. The hadith's own meaning is sufficient without embellishment.

8. Tranquillity in study and remembrance

Muslim 2699a describes tranquillity, mercy and angels surrounding people who gather in a house of Allah to read and study His book. Preserve the setting and the combination of reading and study.

The source compares the Quran with food, water and medicine for the soul. These are metaphors for spiritual need, not biological claims. The Quran offers guidance, but it should not be described as a technical answer to every practical question without study or other knowledge.

9. Protection: distinguish the reports

  • The last two verses of Al-Baqarah: authentic reports describe their sufficiency at night. The meaning of “suffice” has scholarly explanation; do not expand it into immunity from every possible event.
  • The first ten verses of Al-Kahf: Muslim 809a relates protection from the Dajjal in connection with learning these verses.
  • A vessel and fire: the source's statement that a heart containing the Quran will not be punished needs its actual report and explanation. It is not a universal guarantee that every memorizer is beyond accountability.
  • Surah Al-Mulk: reports concern its intercession and protection, with different wordings and assessments. Learn the source before attaching a fixed unconditional result to any reading.

The Quran guides people away from wrongdoing when they respond to it. It does not make a reciter incapable of harm or exempt from consequences.

10. Following the Prophet's example

Quran 33:21 identifies the Messenger as an excellent example for those who hope in Allah and the Last Day and remember Him often. His example includes worship and character, not voice alone.

Aishah's description of his character as the Quran points towards conduct shaped by revelation. The source turns this into a requirement to memorize the entire Quran before acquiring similar virtues. That conclusion does not follow: learn, understand and apply the guidance within your growing knowledge.

11. Ageing and retained worship

The source attributes a protection-from-senility statement to an explanation of Quran 95:5–6. Such commentary needs a clear attribution and assessment. It is not clinical evidence that Quran recitation prevents dementia for every reader.

Older Muslims may experience memory loss despite sincere worship. Continue offering dignity and appropriate support rather than implying that illness reveals spiritual failure. A lifelong reading habit can remain meaningful without a promise that all cognitive decline will be prevented.

12. Grief, anxiety and dua

The Quran and supplication can provide comfort, hope and a language for turning to Allah during distress. Quran 50:16 speaks of Allah's knowledge and nearness in its context; it should not be turned into a guarantee that a reader will never feel anxious.

The source paraphrases the supplication asking that the Quran be the spring of the heart and light of the chest, removing grief and worry. Check the complete report and narration before presenting a shortened paraphrase as the Prophet's exact words. See duas for Quran learning for attribution distinctions.

Depression and anxiety can require professional support. Recitation should not be advertised as eliminating them for everyone or used to discourage treatment.

13. Growth in faith

Quran 8:2 describes believers whose hearts respond when Allah is mentioned and whose faith increases when His verses are recited. Study the verse together with its surrounding description of trust and worship.

A regular reading habit can prompt remembrance and reflection. It is not an automated increase measured solely by pages, and a period of emotional flatness does not by itself prove that no spiritual benefit exists.

14. Light and guidance

The source uses images of light, an unbreakable rope and a straight path to express the Quran's role in guidance. It also cites advice attributed to Jundub about piety and the Quran as light by night and guidance by day, without a precise reference.

Keep that attribution distinct from a verified prophetic report. Reflective descriptions can encourage study, but should not justify the source's additional promise that the Quran cures all diseases or supplies every kind of technical knowledge.

15. A closer relationship with Allah

Reading revelation, worship and supplication can support a person's relationship with Allah. The popular expression about speaking to Allah in prayer and hearing Him through Quran reading is a reflective description, not necessarily a verbatim hadith.

The source says one tear wipes away sins. Repentance is not measured by visible tears alone; sincerity, stopping wrongdoing and addressing rights matter. A person who cannot cry can still repent sincerely.

Ten respectful reading habits

  1. Maintain cleanliness and learn the relevant purification rules for touching a mushaf or reading in another form.
  2. Choose a suitable place and posture that respects the text.
  3. Place the mushaf on a clean support when useful; a pillow is not a universal requirement.
  4. Handle it respectfully; using the right hand should not become an impossible rule for someone with a disability.
  5. Learn istiadhah and the appropriate basmalah rules, including At-Tawbah's exception.
  6. Practise the applicable Tajweed rather than substituting tune for accuracy.
  7. Read at a measured pace that permits correct pronunciation.
  8. Study and respond to the guidance, not only the printed words.
  9. Avoid unnecessary interruptions, while recognising that necessary speech may occur.
  10. Express gratitude without inventing a mandatory completion formula or a fixed reward for it.

How much should you read daily?

There is no single page amount imposed on every person by this general article. Choose a manageable portion and maintain it. A beginner may read a few corrected lines, while a fluent reader can sustain a longer portion. Obligatory recitation within prayer remains a separate matter.

Use a quiet time, record your stopping point and reserve space for meaning. A routine becomes more useful when it continues outside Ramadan as well. The Ramadan reading guide explains completion calculations.

Morning, Fajr and night reading

Reading in the morning can be a helpful habit before work or study, but not every personal preference has a special established reward. Quran 17:78 concerns the witnessed dawn recitation in the prayer context; it should not be broadened to every unrelated morning activity without explanation.

The source quotes the report about Fajr in congregation, remembrance until sunrise and two rakahs. Tirmidhi 586 has those particular elements. It is not a promise for any brief morning reading, and the reward described does not fulfil the obligation of Hajj. Learn the appropriate prayer time after sunrise with a teacher.

At night, recitation may be part of prayer or other worship. The report about fasting and Quran intercession includes night reading, while the report permitting admiration of a person granted Quran mentions its use by night and day. Do not turn these into a requirement to neglect necessary sleep.

Ramadan and Tajweed

Ramadan is connected with revelation and offers a setting for increased study. Reports concerning the restraint of devils do not mean that every distraction or human wrongdoing becomes impossible during the month. Continue learning and correcting mistakes.

Tajweed supports the preservation of letters, lengths and sound relationships. Recitation can be meaningful before a learner understands all Arabic, but translation and Tafsir should accompany progress. The virtues of recitation are not automatically five separate guaranteed outcomes awarded only to someone who knows every technical rule.

Nine reasons to sustain daily engagement

  • Support children's learning and understand the relevant reports about parents.
  • Return to guidance for conduct.
  • Seek principles for responding responsibly to difficulties.
  • Learn distinctions between right and wrong through reliable explanation.
  • Reflect on the signs of Allah described in the text.
  • Seek the reward associated with sincere reading.
  • Respond to commands according to sound knowledge.
  • Value the recitation reward without inventing numerical guarantees.
  • Cultivate humility and compassion through reflection and action.

Ten benefits: a careful summary of the source's list

  1. Honour connected with learning and acting on the Quran.
  2. The Quran's intercession and its role as evidence.
  3. Guidance for life, rather than a technical shortcut for every problem.
  4. Remembrance of Allah and attention to revelation.
  5. The reward described for reading letters.
  6. Possible spiritual comfort without a promised emotional state.
  7. Encouragement towards repentance and forgiveness.
  8. Growth in faithful reflection and practice.
  9. Guidance and established protection reports, rather than immunity from every evil.
  10. Recitation itself as worship.

Health and scientific claims need evidence

The source claims reductions in blood pressure, diabetes, depression and anxiety, prevention of disease and broad brain training. It supplies no clinical studies establishing those outcomes. Do not present the list as scientific proof or tell someone to stop treatment.

Religious comfort and clinical effectiveness are different claims. Quran recitation can retain its spiritual importance without a universal medical promise. The same applies to its conclusion that memorization eliminates boredom, fear and grief or produces a sin-free life.

Read, understand and act

The Quran can be evidence for or against a person, as the source notes; Muslim 223 includes that teaching. Reading should therefore encourage responsibility, not confidence that pronunciation alone excuses neglect of guidance.

Continue with pronunciation, Tajweed, understanding and memorization benefits. Choose a sustainable amount, obtain correction and let the guidance inform your daily conduct.
