Common Mistakes in Online Quran Learning and How to Fix Them

Talqeen Academy · Updated 5 October 2026, 08:35:26 (UTC+03:00)

Most students who stall in Quran learning are not lacking ability. They are repeating a small number of habits that quietly cancel their effort. The good news is that every one of them has a simple fix.

These mistakes are about how we learn, not about recitation errors. For pronunciation and Tajweed errors, see common mistakes in Quranic recitation.

1. Rushing to the Mushaf before the reading foundation is solid

Starting in the middle of a surah feels exciting, but a weak foundation means the same letter and vowel mistakes appear in every line.

Fix: stay with the basics until the teacher confirms the student reads accurately and smoothly. The slower start saves months later. See the beginner roadmap.

2. Skipping revision

New material gets attention; old material quietly fades. This is the most common reason students feel they are "not making progress" while attending every lesson.

Fix: begin every practice session with the previous lesson. In Hifz, never let new memorisation go more than a day or two without recent revision.

3. Practising mistakes

Reading a passage ten times with the same error teaches the error. Repetition only helps once the correct version is known.

Fix: after each lesson, write down the two or three corrections the teacher made and practise only those first.

4. Setting goals that are too big

"Finish a juz this month" sounds motivating, but when it slips the student feels they have failed.

Fix: set a goal for the next two weeks, small enough to be certain. Raise it when it is easy.

5. Studying too many things at once

Tajweed rules, new surahs, Arabic vocabulary and Hifz in the same week split attention and slow all of them.

Fix: one main focus per month, with a light supporting activity at most.

6. Changing teachers or methods too often

Each new teacher restarts the level check and each method uses different terms. Students end up going over the same ground repeatedly.

Fix: give a teacher at least a month of regular lessons before judging. If you do need to change, bring notes on what has been covered.

7. Parents correcting every error at home

Well-meant interruptions make children tense, and they start hiding difficulties.

Fix: leave correction to the teacher. At home, ask the child to read and simply listen. Praise effort and specific progress.

8. Practising when exhausted

A tired student reads sloppily and associates Quran with exhaustion.

Fix: move practice to a time of day when the student is alert, even if it is only ten minutes.

9. Never listening to a model reciter

Pronunciation is learned by ear. Students who only read silently or only with the teacher miss the benefit of hearing correct recitation repeatedly.

Fix: listen to the assigned passage from a reliable reciter before reading it, ideally the one the teacher recommends.

10. No way to see progress

When progress is invisible, motivation fades even if the student is improving.

Fix: keep a simple log: the date, what was covered, what was corrected. Review it monthly. The progress tracking guide shows a ready-made format.
