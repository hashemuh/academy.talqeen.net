Quranic Arabic for Beginners: How to Understand More of the Quran

Talqeen Academy · Updated 5 October 2026, 08:35:26 (UTC+03:00)

Reading the Quran correctly and understanding it are two different skills. Many Muslims can recite beautifully yet follow only a little of the meaning. The good news is that you can start understanding more step by step, without becoming a fluent Arabic speaker first.

This article gives a realistic beginner approach.

Reading and understanding are separate

Reading is decoding the script into sounds. Understanding means knowing the words, how they fit together and what they mean. You can improve the second without waiting for the first to be perfect, though a steady reading ability makes everything easier.

Start with the root system

Most Arabic words are built from a root, usually three letters, with patterns that add meaning. For example, words related to writing share the root k-t-b: kitab (book), katib (writer), maktab (office or desk). Once you notice roots, many words become connected instead of being isolated vocabulary.

Learn common words first

  • Names and attributes of Allah that appear often.
  • Common verbs such as "said", "came" and "believed".
  • Small words like "in", "from", "to", "and", "who" and "that", which hold sentences together.

Because the same words appear repeatedly, learning the frequent ones lets you recognise them across many surahs.

Study a short surah word by word

  1. Read the surah aloud with correct pronunciation.
  2. Look at a word-by-word translation, noting each word's meaning.
  3. Identify roots you recognise from other words.
  4. Read it again with the meaning in mind.
  5. Recite it in prayer and reflect on the meaning as you do.

Starting with surahs you already know by heart makes this easier and more rewarding.

Use a teacher

A teacher can explain grammar when needed, correct misunderstandings and keep you moving at the right pace. Talqeen's Arabic course focuses on real comprehension and communication rather than isolated grammar rules.

Be patient and consistent

A little vocabulary every day adds up. Even five new words a week, with review, gradually transforms how you hear the Quran. For motivation along the way, see motivation tips.
