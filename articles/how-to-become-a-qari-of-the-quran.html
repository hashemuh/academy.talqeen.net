How to Become a Qari: 10 Steps for Developing Quran Recitation

Talqeen Academy · Updated 3 October 2026, 17:52:02 (UTC+03:00)

Developing skilled Quran recitation is a meaningful learning goal that needs dedication and a clear path. The source describes the journey toward becoming a Qari through Tajweed, pronunciation practice, memorization, understanding, and continued guidance.

Structured lessons and regular practice can help you develop those skills. Teaching within your competence, taking part in suitable recitation events, and preparing with a mentor may also form part of a longer learning pathway. An ijazah is a further goal for learners following an authorized teacher's requirements; it should not be treated as a universal definition of the word Qari.

Ten Steps for Developing Your Quran Recitation

The source presents these recommendations as advice from its Quran tutors. The plan should be adapted to your current ability and the recitation you are studying. Start with accuracy, and discuss advanced goals with a qualified mentor.

1. Learn Arabic Sounds and Tajweed Foundations

Tajweed gives you principles for accurate letter sounds and connected recitation. The source names these foundations:

  • Makharij: places of articulation.
  • Sifaat: letter characteristics.
  • Ghunnah: the relevant nasal sound.
  • Qalqalah: the controlled rebounding sound under its conditions.
  • Idgham: applicable merging rules.
  • Madd: the relevant vowel-lengthening rules.

Study with a teacher or a suitable course and practice the rules in short passages. Use our alphabet and Tajweed guide if you need to strengthen the foundation before advancing.

2. Refine Pronunciation through Careful Practice

Learn each letter's sound and articulation, then practice it in words and passages. Begin with a pace slow enough for accuracy. As reading becomes more secure, let the flow improve without forcing speed.

The source mentions Sheikh Al-Afasy as a recitation model and suggests recording your voice to review pronunciation and phrasing. It also names Tarteel as an app to consider for recitation practice. Tools and recordings can support study, while a teacher is needed for the detailed feedback an app or your own listening may not provide.

Regular correction is more useful than a promise of a flawless or professional sound. For practical reading exercises, see our fluency guide.

3. Use a Structured Memorization Plan

The source offers the following example schedule. Adapt the new-learning target to verse length, reading accuracy, retention, and available time.

4. Study Tafsir Alongside Recitation

Reliable explanation helps you engage with a passage's meaning and context. The source suggests beginning with an accessible presentation of a work such as Tafsir Ibn Kathir and focusing on surahs you have learned.

Take notes on key points and attend suitable Tafsir classes online or locally. Understanding can make recitation more thoughtful and help you discuss a passage responsibly, while teaching meanings requires care about the limits of your own knowledge.

5. Choose a Suitable Recitation Institute or Programme

A structured programme can provide a sequence, regular correction, and a teacher who understands your goals. The source recommends Mishkah Academy's live online recitation, Tajweed, Hifz, and ijazah-related programmes.

Those are source-provider recommendations. In your own selection, choose a course matching your level and ask how the teacher corrects errors and reviews progress. A clear foundation should come before advanced delivery or certification goals.

6. Learn with a Qualified Tajweed Teacher

A knowledgeable teacher can hear an error, demonstrate a correction, and ask you to try again. Individual feedback helps you understand which rule or sound needs practice.

The source mentions Sheikh Yasir Qadhi in connection with online Tajweed learning; that mention does not establish a currently available class or booking offer. Find an appropriate local or online teacher, attend regularly, complete assigned practice, and ask questions when an explanation is unclear.

7. Practice and Help Other Learners Responsibly

Explaining something you understand can strengthen your own recall and patience. The source suggests helping children or beginners with basic Tajweed and short surahs, while continuing to correct your own recitation.

Do this within your competence and with appropriate guidance. Repeating a lesson can reveal gaps in your own knowledge, which you should bring back to your teacher rather than pass on unchecked. Helping others should support careful learning for both people.

8. Consider Suitable Recitation Competitions

Preparing for an event can give practice a clear target and offer a chance to meet other learners. The source lists “Global Quran Recitation Competition (GQRC),” “Quranic Open Recitation Competitions,” and “National Quran Recitation Contest” as examples or labels.

These names are retained from the source, rather than presented as verified current events. Check the organizer, eligibility, reading requirements, and schedule of any actual competition before entering.

Prepare through regular recitation and feedback, and use recordings to review your practice. An event can provide motivation and confidence without replacing daily learning or making competitive participation a requirement for every student.

9. Seek Mentorship and Study Relevant Hadith and Sunnah

The source connects advanced preparation with learning about the Prophet's recitation through Hadith and Sunnah. It names Riyad as-Salihin and Sahih al-Bukhari as works to study with suitable guidance.

Attend reliable study circles or classes and learn how relevant narrations relate to Quranic passages. A mentor can help keep those connections accurate and make the learning more meaningful. Advanced recitation preparation should follow the mentor's stated requirements rather than rely on a general checklist alone.

10. Prepare for an Ijazah Assessment with Your Mentor

The source describes an ijazah goal through these steps:

  1. Find a qualified mentor authorized in the area you want to study.
  2. Practice regularly under that mentor's supervision.
  3. Learn the relevant Tajweed and correct recurring mistakes.
  4. Revise memorized material thoroughly where required.
  5. Use practice assessments to identify remaining weaknesses.
  6. Agree the final assessment with the mentor when you are ready.

During an assessment, aim for calm, clear recitation. Any authorization depends on the teacher's evaluation and the scope of the programme. The source presents passing as a route to becoming a Qari with an ijazah; more precisely, ijazah is a specific authorization goal, while “Qari” is used more broadly for a Quran reciter.

Develop Skill through Guided, Consistent Learning

The journey described here combines letter accuracy, Tajweed, memorization, understanding, and ongoing correction. Each stage should prepare you for the next rather than turn advanced certification into an early shortcut.

The original article praises Mishkah Academy's experience and personalized instruction and invites readers to a trial. Evaluate the actual teaching you are offered, keep the practice dependable, and discuss your goals with a mentor. For choosing that support, consult our online Quran teacher guide.
