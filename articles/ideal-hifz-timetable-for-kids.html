16 Hifz Timetable Ideas for Kids: Learning and Revision Plans

Talqeen Academy · Updated 5 October 2026, 08:35:26 (UTC+03:00)

A useful children's Hifz timetable balances new learning with revision, sleep, school and time to rest. A smaller accurate assignment is preferable to a large target that repeatedly causes confusion.

The sixteen options below retain the source's fifteen tables and practical examples. They are starting points for adaptation, not proven formulas that make every child memorize quickly or without stress.

1. A Seven-Day Rhythm

Alternate new assignments with review days so that progress includes retention. A regular place and time can serve as a familiar cue, while the amount should follow checked performance.

DayAssignmentMinutesFocus
1New portion20Begin with a manageable passage
2New portion and review25Check the previous learning
3Revision only20Repair weak passages
4New portion20Extend secure work
5New portion and checking25Assess accurate recall
6Older revision20Return to established material
7Light reading10Keep gentle contact and allow rest

2. An Early-Morning Example

The source proposed 05:00–05:10 for warming up with familiar reading, 05:10–05:25 to learn two to four lines, and 05:25–05:35 for seven to ten repetitions. An additional five-minute check after school revisits the passage.

These are sample clock times, not universal Fajr times. Protect adequate sleep and choose a period when the child is attentive. Water or a small snack may be convenient, but neither this routine nor an early hour guarantees faster retention.

3. A Thirty-to-Forty-Five-Minute Framework

The source used that title, although its actual components add up to forty to forty-five minutes. Shorten the components explicitly if only thirty minutes are available. A short break and a timer can prevent the session running beyond its agreed length.

AssignmentMinutesPurpose
Yesterday’s work10Recall before adding more
Fresh learning15Learn a checked passage
Repeat the new portion10Reinforce the correct sequence
Older work5–10Maintain previous learning

Use the timer to manage time, not to force hurried recitation. Late-night learning is unnecessary if another suitable period is available.

4. Three Short Sessions

Splitting thirty minutes into three visits can fit around school. Use the same fresh portion for the later repetitions rather than adding three different assignments.

PeriodMinutesAssignment
Morning10A small fresh portion
Afternoon10Repeat the same portion
Evening10Check recall and flow

A rest between sessions may help attention. The source also promoted company with other Hifz learners through its classes; verify actual group arrangements instead of assuming every course provides them.

5. Micro-Sessions for a Full Day

The source outlined four small activities: listening, repeating one or two lines, reading slowly from the Mushaf and testing recall. Each can take three to five minutes. Its three-slot table combines reading and repetition in the middle slot.

PeriodMinutesAssignment
Morning3Listen to an accurate recording
Short break4Read slowly and repeat
Evening5Try recall without looking

A consistent reliable recording can provide a stable pronunciation model. Familiar melody alone is not proof of accurate memorization.

6. Morning Learning and Evening Revision

This version separates fresh work from later checking. Begin with a weak familiar surah when needed, learn the new portion and finish with a slow corrected reading.

SessionMinutesAssignment
Morning20–25New learning with checked repetition
Evening15–20Older review and corrections

The gap creates another opportunity to recall the passage. Adjust the times on school days and weekends rather than assuming this division suits every family.

7. Revision Before New Material

Give older work priority when forgetting has increased. The example uses fifteen minutes for older material, ten for yesterday's portion and five for new work only when earlier review is stable.

LayerPriorityAssignment
Older memorizationHighReview fifteen minutes
Yesterday’s passageMediumReview ten minutes
New workOnly after secure reviewTry five minutes when ready

One carefully corrected passage can be more useful than several hurried attempts. The purpose is accuracy, not a universal numerical comparison of slow and fast practice.

8. Layers of Review

Distinguish fresh, recent and older memorization. The repetition counts below are examples, changed according to errors and secure recall.

LayerExample repetitionsAim
Fresh passage5Check precise wording
Recent passage3Reconnect the sequence
Older passage2Test continuing recall

For a daily five-to-ten-minute spot check, select three to five ayahs from different learned portions or juz. Recite at a natural pace with appropriate breathing and stops. Record slips for later correction rather than demanding uninterrupted recitation.

A weekly thirty-to-sixty-minute review can revisit the previous seven days, mark weak points, connect a known surah and end with slower checking. Divide a longer review into breaks where the child needs them.

9. An Exam-Week Maintenance Plan

During demanding school weeks, temporarily pause fresh learning and maintain a smaller review. This preserves contact with the Quran without treating exam pressure as a reason to push the same workload.

AssignmentMinutesPurpose
Older material5Recall what is already learned
Slow repetition5Repair a selected passage
Listening5Revisit an accurate model

The source promised no memorization loss; no timetable establishes that. Keep checking recall and resume new assignments when circumstances allow.

10. A Holiday Routine

More available time can permit three visits to the material, with spaces between them. The example totals fifty minutes, but an individual child may need less.

PeriodMinutesAssignment
Morning25Fresh learning
Afternoon15Repetition and correction
Evening10Older material

Use a fresh and attentive period for the main assignment. “Night” in the source does not require delaying bedtime; an earlier evening review may be more suitable. Extra sessions are not a guarantee against burnout.

11. A Gentle Example for Ages Five to Seven

A young learner may begin with listening, a few repetitions and familiar review. The age range is an illustration; reading ability, readiness and temperament matter more than age alone.

AssignmentMinutesApproach
Listening5Follow a small passage
Repeating5Use gentle, checked practice
Tiny review5Return to familiar material

Keep encouragement warm and the passage small. A child who needs a shorter session should not be forced to complete fifteen minutes merely because a table lists it.

12. A Packed-Schedule Alternative

On unusually busy days, this seventeen-minute maintenance pattern leaves out fresh learning. Use it to revisit familiar material calmly.

PeriodMinutesAssignment
Morning7Revision without fresh work
After school5Brief reading check
Evening5Listen to an accurate recording

Listening complements recall but does not replace an assessed recitation. Return to a wider revision plan when time becomes available.

13. Short Tasks and Movement Breaks

The source labelled this an ADHD timetable. It is an adaptable learning example, not a treatment or a validated plan for every child with ADHD. NHS guidance includes simple instructions, divided tasks and breaks among possible supports. Discuss appropriate adjustments with those supporting the child's learning.

StageMinutesAssignment
Fresh work5One small, clear task
Break2Move or rest
Repetition5Return to the checked passage
Revision5Review familiar material

A familiar chair, reliable recording and short timer may help organise the task. Follow the child's existing support arrangements and revise the intervals when they are unsuitable.

14. Travel-Day Practice

Travel can offer an optional listening opportunity when the child is comfortable. Repetition and an evening recall check complete the source's three-stage example.

Travel stageAssignmentFocus
Outward journeyListen when comfortableFollow the assigned passage
Return journeyRepeat when suitableRecall familiar material
At home in the eveningBrief recall testIdentify corrections

A five-minute check at home can reveal what needs correction. Audio exposure during a journey should not be counted automatically as a new memorized assignment.

The suggested support routine was ten minutes listening to the new portion, ten addressing errors and five revisiting older work, followed by a Sunday review of the record. A parent who cannot verify a pronunciation should note it for the teacher rather than invent a correction.

16. A Four-Week Cycle

Choose a modest monthly goal, such as a suitable number of lines or a surah. Weekly records can show whether to continue, reduce fresh work or allocate another review week.

WeekPriorityPurpose
1Begin a new targetEstablish a suitable assignment
2Strengthen weak partsCorrect recurring slips
3Connect the whole surahCheck sequence and flow
4Review and consolidateAssess dependable recall

A modest end-of-month reward can recognise effort and improved accuracy. Reaching an assigned target is not more important than making the learning experience sustainable.

Making the Timetable Work

Agree the assignment with a qualified teacher, track mistakes and review the routine as the child develops. The source advertised an especially effective programme with expert support; its claims should not be transferred to another provider without evidence.

Consult course information, children's memorization methods, page-based schedule calculations and revision guidance.
