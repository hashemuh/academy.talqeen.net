Online Tajweed Classes for Adults: Learn to Recite Correctly

Talqeen Academy · Updated 3 October 2026

Many adults read the Quran every day yet feel unsure about their pronunciation. They learned as children, or picked it up later, and nobody ever corrected the details. Tajweed lessons are for exactly this: to refine recitation until it is accurate and confident.

Online lessons suit adults because they remove the commute and fit around work and family.

What Tajweed covers

  • Makharij: where each letter is pronounced from in the mouth and throat.
  • Sifat: the qualities of letters, such as heaviness and lightness.
  • Rules of noon sakinah and tanween: how they are pronounced before different letters.
  • Madd: how long vowels are elongated.
  • Ghunnah and qalqalah: nasal sound and echoing sound.
  • Stopping and starting: where it is correct to pause.

How an online Tajweed lesson works

The teacher listens to you recite a passage, notes the mistakes and explains the rule behind each. You then repeat the corrected version until it is natural. Because a rule applies throughout the Quran, learning it once improves everything you read afterwards. The Tajweed course at Talqeen is structured around this listen-correct-repeat approach.

Obstacles adults face

  • Habit. Years of reading in a particular way means mistakes are automatic. Change takes repetition.
  • Sounds from other languages. Some Arabic letters have no equivalent in your first language and need patient practice.
  • Self-consciousness. Reciting aloud to a teacher can feel vulnerable. Remember that teachers hear this every day and want you to succeed.
  • Time. Short daily practice works better than long, occasional sessions.

A realistic plan

  1. Level check: the teacher hears you recite and identifies your main habits.
  2. Makharij first: fix the sounds of the letters you mispronounce most.
  3. Core rules: noon sakinah, madd and ghunnah, applied to passages you already know.
  4. Fluent application: recite longer passages applying all rules together.

Between lessons, use the home practice plan, focusing on the corrections your teacher gives.

Start with surahs you know

Practising rules on familiar surahs, such as those you recite in prayer, means you can focus on how you say them rather than on reading them. Corrections then carry directly into your daily prayer.
