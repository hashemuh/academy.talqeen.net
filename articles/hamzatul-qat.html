Hamzat al-Qat: Pronunciation, Writing and Quran Examples

Talqeen Academy · Updated 10 October 20265 October 2026, 08:35:26 (UTC+03:00) · Tajweed

Hamzat al-qat is a hamzah pronounced when beginning its word and in ordinary connected speech. Hamzat al-wasl is pronounced when starting from its word but normally omitted when joining from the preceding word. The distinction helps learners read Arabic and the Quran accurately.

The source described a “cutting” hamzah as interrupting speech. The term identifies a sound, not an instruction to insert a breath or dramatic break. Pronounce it clearly without force or arbitrary emphasis.

Definition and Reading Scope

Hamzah is a glottal consonant, distinct from the long-vowel alif that may support its written form. The starting-versus-joining distinction is the central beginner rule. Claims that it can never undergo any specialised recitation treatment are too absolute across all transmissions; follow the particular reading taught.

For the general distinction, see Quran Academy's hamzah lesson. Recognition should lead to actual spoken examples with feedback.

Rules and Word Examples

Beginning and Connected Speech

The initial hamzah of أَحْمَد remains in ordinary connected reading, such as رَأَيْتُ أَحْمَدَ. The source also used أَعْلَمُ, “I know”, as an initial example. A teacher can demonstrate the difference between retaining this sound and omitting a joining hamzah.

Nouns, Verbs and Particles

Noun examples include أَمَل, hope; أَسَد, lion; and إِنْسَان, human being. Verb examples include أَكَلَ, he ate, and أُحِبُّ, I love. Particle examples include إِنَّ and أَنْ. The presence of initial hamzah must be checked in the actual form, not inferred solely from the word class.

Written Seats

The source incorrectly described أُسْتَاذ as hamzah on waw and إِيمَان or إِنْسَان as hamzah on ya. All of those initial examples use an alif seat: hamzah appears above alif with fathah or dammah and below it with kasrah.

أَمَامَ also begins with hamzah above alif. Hamzah elsewhere can appear on waw, on a ya-shaped seat or on the line, under spelling rules that consider more than only the following vowel. Learn those separately from the initial qat-versus-wasl distinction.

Clear Sound without Strain

Use an accurate model and repeat at a comfortable pace. “Strong emphasis” should not mean tightening the throat excessively or making hamzah heavy. Clarity comes from the correct sound and vowel.

Correcting the Quran Examples

Al-Fatihah

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

The source called the hamzah of الْحَمْدُ qat and labelled this line 1:1. In the common Hafs numbering it is 1:2, and the article al- begins with hamzat al-wasl. The phrase is retained as a contrasting example, not as proof of qat.

Al-Ikhlas 112:1

قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ

أَحَدٌ contains a pronounced initial hamzat al-qat. In ordinary connected reading it remains audible after the preceding word. The initial sound associated with Allah when starting is a different joining-hamzah question.

Al-Baqarah 2:255

اللَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ

The initial hamzah in إِلَٰهَ is qat; إِلَّا provides another example. The quotation is the opening portion of Ayat al-Kursi, not the entire ayah.

Qat and Wasl Compared

  • Pronunciation: qat remains in ordinary joining; wasl normally drops when connected from a preceding word.
  • Writing: initial qat is commonly أ or إ, while wasl may be a plain alif or carry the Quranic wasl sign ٱ.
  • Word structure: omitting wasl in speech does not mean its printed alif automatically disappears.
  • Purpose: the distinction supports correct joining and beginning, not deliberate dramatic interruption.

Special orthographic and recitation cases require separate instruction. Avoid turning a beginner summary into an absolute rule covering every qiraah and spelling environment.

Practising the Distinction

Read a selected word alone, then in a short connected phrase. Ask a teacher to hear whether the hamzah is retained or omitted appropriately. Compare print with sound and note any uncertainty before extensive repetition.
