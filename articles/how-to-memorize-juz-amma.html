How to Memorize Juz Amma: A Corrected Study Plan

Talqeen Academy · Updated 10 October 20265 October 2026, 08:35:26 (UTC+03:00) · Hifz

Juz Amma is the thirtieth juz, covering An-Naba through An-Nas: chapters 78–114. Its thirty-seven surahs include many shorter chapters useful for beginner practice. A learning plan should combine accurate reading, manageable new work and regular revision.

The source's eight methods included scheduling, starting with An-Nas, colour cues, patterns, meanings, small sections, weekly review and prayer practice. Several factual examples needed correction, particularly the completion estimate and which surahs belong to this juz.

Eight Methods for Learning Juz Amma

1. Plan New Work and Revision

Choose a passage based on available time, reading ability and the amount already learned. Include breaks and a progress record. The source claimed five ayahs daily would finish the juz in about twenty days. With 564 ayahs in the common numbering, that rate requires 113 new-learning days before allowing for revision or missed days. Ayah lengths also vary considerably.

2. Consider An-Nas as a Starting Point

An-Nas has six ayahs and can be a manageable beginning. It is not the shortest surah, contrary to the source claim: Al-Kawthar has three ayahs and fewer words. A teacher can choose An-Nas for familiarity or another short passage suited to the learner.

3. Use Colour Coding with Its Legend

A colour-coded mushaf may remind a learner of a rule already understood. The source chart is preserved below as its illustrative scheme, not a universal system. Publishers assign colours differently; check the edition rather than assuming green or red always means the same rule.

4. Notice Structure and Repeated Wording

Study a surah's actual sequence and recurring expressions. The source generalised that many begin with a call to worship or mercy; openings differ, so use checked examples rather than imposing one pattern. Structure can help recall but does not replace word-by-word accuracy.

5. Understand the Themes

SurahTheme for introductory study
An-NasSeeking refuge from the whisperer, from jinn and people
Al-FalaqSeeking refuge from harms, darkness and envy
Al-IkhlasAllah's oneness and uniqueness
An-NasrAllah's help and victory, followed by praise and seeking forgiveness

An-Nasr is not a general promise that every worldly goal follows from faith. Use a reliable explanation to connect each theme with its wording.

6. Divide Longer Passages Carefully

Learn a small section, test it and practise joining it to the next. The source gave Al-Mulk, Al-Muddaththir and Al-Mursalat as longer Juz Amma examples. Those belong to Juz Tabarak, the twenty-ninth juz, so they are retained here as a corrected comparison. Within Juz Amma, An-Naba or An-Naziat can illustrate longer beginner tasks.

7. Keep a Weekly Review Checkpoint

Recite the week's material, identify weak places and revisit them. Weekly consolidation complements shorter daily review; it should not become the only return to a newly learned surah. Have the teacher assess recall, not just the number of pages read.

8. Use Familiar Surahs in Prayer Appropriately

The source suggested Al-Fatihah, Al-Ikhlas, An-Nas and Al-Falaq for prayer practice. Al-Fatihah is outside Juz Amma and has its own role in salah. Practise accurately beforehand and seek qualified guidance on prayer-specific questions. Prayer can reinforce learning without becoming a pressured memory test.

How Long Does Memorisation Take?

The source described fifteen to twenty days as typical. No completion data supports that claim. Such a short period would require a substantial daily load and is unsuitable as a general beginner expectation. Choose a pace that includes pronunciation, retained recall and review.

Personal feedback can help you identify reading errors and weak recall while memorizing Juz Amma. Combine manageable targets with a revision routine and ask a teacher how progress will be checked. Related guidance: Hifz foundations, revision methods, course information.
