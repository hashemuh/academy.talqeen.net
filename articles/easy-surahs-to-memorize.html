18 Short Surahs to Begin Quran Memorization

Talqeen Academy · Updated 10 October 20265 October 2026, 08:35:26 (UTC+03:00) · Hifz

Short surahs can provide a manageable beginning to Hifz because a learner can focus on a limited passage and return to it frequently. Ease still depends on pronunciation, ayah length, familiarity and the quality of instruction.

Most of the source's selections are in Juz Amma. Al-Fatihah is the opening chapter in Juz 1, not Juz Amma. The eighteen examples below retain their chapter numbers, standard ayah counts and themes while avoiding a universal ranking of difficulty.

Eighteen Surahs to Consider

1. Al-Fatihah — Chapter 1, Seven Ayahs

The opening surah combines praise, worship and a request for guidance. Its place in Salah makes accurate learning an important foundation. Repeated use can support familiarity, but mistakes should be checked rather than repeated.

2. Al-Ikhlas — Chapter 112, Four Ayahs

This compact surah teaches Allah's oneness and uniqueness. Its short passage can connect belief with careful recitation. It is also part of the bedtime recitation reported in Bukhari 5017.

3. Al-Falaq — Chapter 113, Five Ayahs

Al-Falaq expresses seeking Allah's protection from several forms of harm. A learner can study its requests together and practise the sounds accurately. The source noted its use in morning, evening and bedtime devotional practice.

4. An-Nas — Chapter 114, Six Ayahs

The final chapter seeks protection from harmful whispering. Its recurring structure may aid recall. Learning it alongside Al-Falaq can be practical, while keeping the two texts distinct.

5. Al-Kawthar — Chapter 108, Three Ayahs

Al-Kawthar is the shortest surah and concerns Allah's gift to the Prophet, worship and the fate of his adversary. A small complete passage can encourage a beginner, without promising immediate or stress-free memorization.

6. Al-Asr — Chapter 103, Three Ayahs

Its concise passage addresses time, human loss, faith, good deeds, truth and patience. Understand the sequence as well as the sounds. Three ayahs do not mean that every pronunciation will already be easy.

7. Al-Maun — Chapter 107, Seven Ayahs

This surah connects religious accountability with treatment of others and the sincerity of prayer. Study its references to the orphan, feeding the poor and small assistance, rather than reducing it to a general kindness slogan.

8. Al-Fil — Chapter 105, Five Ayahs

The account of the People of the Elephant offers a short narrative sequence. A teacher can explain the events simply and help a child recall the order without adding invented historical details.

9. Quraysh — Chapter 106, Four Ayahs

Quraysh addresses provision, safety and worship of the Lord of the House. Its thematic relationship with Al-Fil may support study, but each remains a separate surah.

10. At-Takathur — Chapter 102, Eight Ayahs

The passage warns against absorbing rivalry in worldly increase and reminds people of accountability. Repeated phrasing can provide cues when the learner keeps the exact word sequence clear.

11. Al-Humazah — Chapter 104, Nine Ayahs

It warns about slander, mockery and reliance on accumulated wealth. Short ayahs can be practised individually before the whole passage is joined and checked.

12. Al-Qariah — Chapter 101, Eleven Ayahs

This surah describes the striking event of judgement and the weighing of deeds. Repeated expressions may help familiarity, but attention to transitions remains necessary.

13. Az-Zalzalah — Chapter 99, Eight Ayahs

The surah describes the earth's upheaval and the disclosure of deeds, including the smallest good and evil. Connect the narrative and accountability themes while checking each ending.

14. At-Tin — Chapter 95, Eight Ayahs

Beginning with oaths, it addresses human creation, faith and judgement. Its short divisions and memorable opening may support practice; the meanings still deserve explanation.

15. Ash-Sharh — Chapter 94, Eight Ayahs

The repeated teaching about ease with hardship provides a recognisable pattern. Keep the exact repetitions and surrounding lines secure, and revisit the surah in daily review.

16. Ad-Duha — Chapter 93, Eleven Ayahs

It reassures the Prophet and connects remembered blessings with care for others. Learn the sequence gradually, including its concluding guidance concerning the orphan, the petitioner and Allah's favour.

17. Al-Layl — Chapter 92, Twenty-One Ayahs

The longer selection contrasts people's striving and choices concerning giving and righteousness. Repeated patterns can assist learning in sections. It is a possible next stage rather than automatically an easy first assignment.

18. Al-Bayyinah — Chapter 98, Eight Ayahs

This chapter discusses clear proof, worship and the outcomes of belief and disbelief. Several ayahs are longer than those in very short surahs, so the count alone should not determine the target.

Why Begin with Short Passages?

A smaller assignment can feel more achievable, permit frequent review and allow a learner to experience completing a surah. Familiar prayer recitation can provide additional practice. These practical advantages do not establish that every short surah has simple language or that any learner will retain it without revision.

The source advertised qualified children's Hifz tutors and effortless progress. Those claims belong to its provider. Check actual course arrangements before selecting instruction.
