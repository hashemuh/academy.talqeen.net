A Two-Year Quran Memorization Plan: Targets, Practice, and Revision

Talqeen Academy · Updated 3 October 2026, 18:23:15 (UTC+03:00)

Some learners choose two years as a target for Quran memorization. A structured plan can help organize that goal, but the timetable should be checked against your starting level, daily availability, and ability to retain earlier passages.

The source suggests starting with shorter surahs in Juz Amma, using 2–3 study periods each day, reserving at least two hours for Hifz, practicing suitable memorization techniques, and reviewing learned passages in Salah. It also recommends a programme and a supportive group. These are planning ideas, not a guarantee that every student will complete Hifz within two years.

How to Interpret the Numbers

The calculation is 10 × 26 × 24 = 6,240. The source also uses 6,236 as the ayah count, so its target is four higher than that count. The table is a planning calculation rather than proof of completion: ayahs differ greatly in length, and secure retention must be assessed.

The original table labels its four revision days as Sundays. Actual months and Sunday counts vary, so plan using your real calendar. Dedicated review days should supplement daily revision rather than leave older memorization untouched between them.

Eight Tips for Working toward the Goal

Use these tips to discuss a realistic plan with your tutor. A fixed date should serve the learning process rather than require you to ignore weak recitation or recall.

1. Begin with Short Surahs in Juz Amma

Shorter passages can give a learner manageable first targets and help establish the practice routine. Many are familiar from Salah and can be revisited during prayer after accurate learning.

Starting with small portions also gives you time to develop repetition and review habits before tackling longer surahs. See where to begin Hifz and techniques for learning a surah.

2. Use Two or Three Study Periods Daily

The source suggests Fajr, Asr, and Isha as possible anchors for study. You might use the morning for new material, an afternoon period for revision, and the evening to reinforce the day's work.

These times are suggestions, not a requirement that concentration is identical for everyone. Choose dependable periods that fit your responsibilities and keep the purpose of each session clear.

3. Reserve Substantial Daily Study Time

The source proposes at least two hours daily for this intensive target, divided into shorter sessions where useful. The time is intended to include new learning and review rather than two hours of new ayahs alone.

Two hours do not automatically make the schedule achievable. Track what you can learn accurately and retain, then discuss whether the target needs more time or a longer overall timetable. Dividing practice can help avoid an overly tiring single session.

4. Learn with Supportive Fellow Students or Huffaz

A group can offer motivation and accountability in local or online learning. Students can discuss methods, practice recall, and support regular review together.

Peer practice should complement qualified correction. A shared goal can encourage consistency without turning a learner's pace into pressure to match everyone else.

5. Choose a Consistent Memorization Method

The source names four methods or labels:

  • 3.10 Hifz technique: Presented as a repetition-based approach; ask the tutor to explain the exact sequence being used.
  • Chunking: Divide longer passages into manageable portions, then join them.
  • 3×3 method: Use a defined repetition sequence to practice ayahs and their connections.
  • Ottoman method: The source connects this label with using the Mushaf layout as a memory aid. Confirm the precise method with the teacher, since naming conventions vary.

Choose an approach that helps your own recall and apply it consistently. None of these names removes the need for daily review or guarantees that a passage will never be forgotten.

6. Protect Tajweed during Memorization

Correct sounds need attention before repeated memory practice. Makharij and the appropriate application of ghunnah are examples of the pronunciation work named in the source.

The article cites: “And recite the Quran with measured recitation” — Surah Al-Muzzammil, 73:4.

Learn the passage carefully with a teacher and review corrections. Accurate pronunciation supports recitation in lessons, prayers, and other settings. See a pathway for correct Quran reading.

7. Revisit Learned Passages in Salah

Reciting accurately learned passages in prayer can make review part of a regular act of worship. The source also mentions longer voluntary prayers, such as Tahajjud, as opportunities to revisit memorization.

Use this as part of the revision routine rather than your only assessment. A tutor should still hear the passage and identify errors you may not recognize while reciting alone.

8. Plan Weekly and Monthly Revision

Weekly review can check recently learned material, while a broader monthly cycle returns to earlier passages. Keep new material and old review in balance as the memorized amount increases.

Use specific review days and a progress record to identify weak sections. A Hifz chart can help organize these tasks, while regular oral testing shows what is actually retained.

Keep the Timetable Accountable to Retention

A two-year Hifz target needs substantial effort and an adaptable plan. Self-practice is essential, and an experienced tutor can provide correction, revision structure, and a realistic assessment of progress.

The source recommends Mishkah Academy's tutors and children's Hifz programmes and names learners in the UK, USA, Canada, Australia, Germany, and other European countries as audiences for its support. Its claims of expertise and efficient completion belong to that provider and should be checked against the actual course offered.

Choose a timetable that preserves accuracy and recall. If responsibilities or retention make the two-year goal unsuitable, a longer plan can be the better learning decision. Read ways to balance Hifz with a busy schedule before committing to an intensive pace.
