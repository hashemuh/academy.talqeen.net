How to Choose the Best Online Quran Academy

Talqeen Academy · Updated 10 October 20265 October 2026, 08:35:26 (UTC+03:00) · Quran Reading

Choosing an academy is different from choosing an individual tutor. An academy brings structure: a curriculum, a way of matching students with teachers, a system for scheduling and billing and a process if something goes wrong. These are the things to compare.

If you are looking at a single tutor instead, see how to find a reliable Quran tutor online.

1. The teaching format

Find out whether lessons are live and one-to-one, live in groups or recorded. For recitation, a teacher needs to hear and correct the student, which favours live one-to-one lessons. Recorded content can complement lessons but does not replace correction.

2. Teacher qualifications

  • Are teachers qualified native Arabic speakers?
  • Do the Quran teachers hold an Ijazah (a certificate of recitation)?
  • Do they have experience teaching your age group?
  • Does the academy vet teachers, or is it an open marketplace?

Talqeen Academy states that its teachers are qualified native Arab speakers, many with Ijazah, and that it does not hire untrained tutors. Ask any academy to explain its own standards.

3. A real free trial

A trial lesson reveals more than any website. Check that it is a genuine lesson with a real teacher, with no hidden commitment. Use the free trial checklist to judge it.

4. Method and curriculum

A good academy can describe how it teaches: how beginners are introduced to letters and sounds, how Tajweed is layered in, how Hifz and revision are structured. Talqeen's teaching methodology, for example, describes a harakah-first reading approach, personalised pacing and mastery before advancing.

5. Transparent pricing

  • Is the price per hour clear?
  • Are there extra fees?
  • What payment methods and currencies are accepted?
  • What is the policy for missed or cancelled lessons?

Convert all offers into a price per hour so you can compare fairly. At the time of writing, Talqeen Academy's price is $10 per hour across courses; check the pricing page for current terms.

6. Scheduling and time zones

Check whether the academy has teachers available at your times, especially if you live in the USA, UK, Canada or Australia and the teacher is in another time zone. Weekend and early-morning options matter for working adults and school children.

7. Progress reporting and communication

Ask how you will be told about progress, how often, and whom to contact if you have a concern. Regular, specific feedback is a sign of a well-run academy.

8. Child safety

For children, ask about the academy's safeguarding practices, such as who may contact families and how, and whether lessons are recorded. A good academy answers directly and welcomes parents being present.

A simple scorecard

Score each academy from 1 to 5 on format, teachers, trial, method, pricing, scheduling, reporting and safety. A low score on any one of teachers, trial or safety should count more than a high score elsewhere.
