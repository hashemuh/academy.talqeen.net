How to Help Kids Stop Forgetting the Surahs of Juz Amma

Talqeen Academy · Updated 3 October 2026

It is a familiar frustration: your child learned a surah well, and a few weeks later it is half gone. This is not a sign of a poor memory. Memorisation fades without revision, and children revise less naturally than adults.

The solution is to build revision into the routine so that it takes little effort.

In this article

  1. Why memorised surahs fade
  2. Revise at spaced intervals
  3. Use prayer as built-in revision
  4. Link surahs together
  5. Make recall active, not passive
  6. Keep a revision list
  7. Bring the teacher in
  8. Frequently asked questions

Why memorised surahs fade

New material feels strong right after learning because it is in short-term memory. It becomes long-term only through repeated recall over time. Without that, even well-learned surahs slip, especially shorter ones that children recite quickly and then stop thinking about.

Revise at spaced intervals

  1. The day after learning a surah.
  2. Three or four days later.
  3. A week later.
  4. Then every couple of weeks, then monthly.

You do not need a perfect schedule. Simply putting each new surah on a list and revisiting it a few times is enough.

Use prayer as built-in revision

Reciting different surahs in daily prayers is one of the best revision habits. Encourage your child to choose a different surah from the ones they know for each prayer. It gives real purpose to what they learned, and regular repetition.

Link surahs together

Reciting several surahs in a row, in their order in the Mushaf, helps one verse trigger the next and strengthens the sequence. A bedtime recitation of two or three surahs works well.

Make recall active, not passive

  • Ask the child to recite without looking, then check.
  • Play "finish the verse": you start, the child completes.
  • Have them write a surah from memory if they can write Arabic.
  • Let them teach a younger sibling or you.

Active recall strengthens memory far more than rereading.

Keep a revision list

Write the surahs the child knows on a card or chart and tick each when revised. Children love seeing the ticks grow, and parents can see at a glance which surahs have not been touched for a while.

Bring the teacher in

In Hifz lessons, a teacher will usually include recent and older revision (sabqi and manzil) alongside new material. Ask for the revision to be a set part of each lesson. For a broader view, see Quran memorisation at home.
