A Simple Home Practice Plan Between Quran Lessons

Talqeen Academy · Updated 5 October 2026, 08:35:26 (UTC+03:00)

What you do between lessons matters as much as the lessons. Without practice, the corrections made in class fade by the next session and the teacher ends up repeating the same points.

This is a practice plan for the sessions themselves: what to do in those ten to fifteen minutes. For when to do them across the week, see the weekly routine.

What you need

  • The assigned page or passage, in the same mushaf or book used in lessons.
  • A notebook and pencil for corrections.
  • A timer. Setting 10 or 15 minutes removes the question of "how long should I do this?"
  • A recording of a reliable reciter, if the teacher recommends one.

The four-step session

  1. Listen (2 minutes). Play the assigned passage once and follow along with your eyes. Notice how the reciter handles the parts you find difficult.
  2. Read slowly (4 minutes). Read aloud at a pace you can control. Do not rush; accuracy first, speed later.
  3. Fix the corrections (4 minutes). Open the notes from your last lesson and practise the two or three corrections the teacher made, on their own and in context.
  4. Record or recite once (1-2 minutes). Say the passage once without stopping, or record it. This is your check, and your evidence of progress.

A day-by-day example

Assume lessons on Monday, Wednesday and Friday.

DayPractice focus
Mon (lesson day)Short read-through afterwards while it is fresh
TueFull four-step session on Monday's material
Wed (lesson day)Warm up before class by reading the previous assignment
ThuFull four-step session on Wednesday's material
Fri (lesson day)Warm up, then relax after class
SatLight listening, and one recorded recitation of the week's best passage
SunRest, or a five-minute review of your corrections notebook

For children: how a parent can help

  • Sit nearby while the child practises. You do not need to correct anything.
  • Let the child choose the order of the first two steps.
  • Celebrate the effort: "I heard you fix that word on your own."
  • Stop at the timer, even if the child wants to continue or has not "finished".

For adults: make it fit

Choose a fixed trigger: after Fajr, on the commute with headphones for the listening step, or after dinner. If you only have five minutes, do steps 1 and 3. Short and consistent beats long and occasional.

Keep a corrections notebook

One line per correction: the word, what went wrong, the right way. Review it before each lesson. Over a few weeks, you will see which mistakes have gone and which are stubborn, and you can ask the teacher for targeted help.

If a session goes badly

Some days nothing works. Finish with one clean reading of a short, familiar passage so the session ends on success, and move on. Tell the teacher next lesson what felt difficult.
