How Long Does It Take to Recite the Quran? Time and Page Planning

Talqeen Academy · Updated 5 October 2026, 08:35:26 (UTC+03:00)

Completing a Quran recitation depends on accurate reading pace, available time, pauses and the purpose of study. Reading Arabic aloud, studying a translation and discussing tafsir are different activities and should not be assigned one identical duration.

The source suggested twenty to twenty-five total hours, three to four months for a moderate daily routine, and elsewhere two to four months. These are unverified general estimates, not a measured average for most readers. A short timed sample of your own accurate reading gives a more useful starting point.

Estimate Total Recitation Time from Your Pace

Choose a representative passage, read at a measured pace and record the time. Repeat with different passages before extrapolating. Include pauses and correction rather than trying to produce an impressive reading speed.

The Quran contains 114 surahs and thirty juz. A familiar Madani edition has 604 pages; the source’s approximately 600 is a planning approximation, not the page count of every copy. Multiply your measured time per page by your edition’s total, with room for variation.

How Long Does One Juz Take?

The source proposed forty-five to sixty minutes for a juz, with approximately twenty pages. Thirty juz at that rate gives 22.5–30 hours, which is a different estimate from its twenty-to-twenty-five-hour total. Neither is a guaranteed rate.

It also claimed an average of 148 ayahs per juz. Using the commonly printed Kufan count of 6,236, the arithmetic average is about 207.9. Individual juz differ, and ayah lengths vary greatly, so a verse count is not a consistent time unit.

One juz each day gives thirty daily portions. A slower reader or someone studying meaning may need more time, while a fluent reciter’s actual pace should still preserve proper delivery.

Daily Time Examples for Different Responsibilities

The source framed one example as a boy or student with thirty to forty-five minutes and ten to fifteen pages daily. Use this for any learner with suitable availability. At ten to fifteen pages, a 604-page copy takes approximately sixty-one to forty-one reading days, not automatically one month.

Its household-care example suggested twenty to thirty minutes after Fajr or before bed and half a juz daily. This can apply to anyone with caregiving duties; the portion produces roughly sixty daily sessions, provided the actual reading time permits it.

Its worker example suggested fifteen to twenty minutes morning and evening, splitting one juz between sessions. Confirm your pace: thirty to forty minutes may not cover a juz for everyone. Adjust around the workload rather than treating occupational or gender categories as learning-rate predictions.

Why Completion Times Differ

Work, education and family commitments change available study time. Unfamiliar Arabic, uncertain sounds and the need for correction also affect pace. Native Arabic speakers can need specialised recitation guidance, so do not assume language background alone predicts accuracy.

Reflection and explanation add a different kind of study. A two-to-four-month timetable may suit some routines, but it is not established as the duration for most people. Maintain a measured reading and ask qualified teachers or scholars about unclear words and meanings.

Guidance against Rushing

The source cited Abu Dawud 1390. The report recounts Abdullah ibn Amr asking about completion, an initial answer of a month, further discussion reaching seven days, and a warning concerning understanding when completion is in fewer than three days.

That report encourages attention rather than a race. It should not be turned into one compulsory timetable for all circumstances. Ask qualified guidance about detailed interpretation and keep accuracy and reflection central to your personal plan.

A Corrected Thirty-Day Page Schedule

The original table allocated four pages after each prayer for one completion, six for two and twelve for three. Over thirty days, these produce 600, 900 and 1,800 pages. Six per prayer therefore covers only one and a half 600-page copies, not two.

For a 604-page copy, add four pages per completion: four for one, eight for two or twelve for three beyond those totals. Alternatively, follow one actual juz daily for a single completion and track its real boundaries. Page references vary by edition.

These are optional study allocations around prayer, not prescribed religious page quotas. Multiple monthly completions require a substantial workload; reduce the target if it compromises measured recitation or essential responsibilities.

Improve Reading through Appropriate Support

The source recommended a tutor for correction, encouragement, Tajweed and meaning. Confirm competence in each subject: recitation training does not automatically establish expertise in tafsir. Ask for precise feedback and apply it before increasing speed.

A beginner may start with Noorani Qaida or another suitable primer, learn vowel patterns and joining, and apply the lessons in short surahs. See the initial reading guide. Tajweed should preserve accurate sounds rather than become merely an aesthetic goal.

Use daily schedule planning and a reading roadmap. The source’s closing claim that Mishkah’s teachers guarantee recitation and deep understanding is not a verified ranking or Talqeen promise. Confirm actual instruction through course information.
