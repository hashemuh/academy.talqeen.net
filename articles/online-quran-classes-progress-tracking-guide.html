How to Track Progress in Online Quran Classes

Talqeen Academy · Updated 3 October 2026

It is hard to feel progress in Quran learning because improvement is gradual and often invisible: a word that used to be a struggle is now automatic, and nobody notices the day it changed. Tracking makes progress visible, which keeps students and parents motivated and helps the teacher plan.

Tracking does not need to be complicated. A short note after each lesson and a monthly review is enough.

What to measure

  • Accuracy: how many mistakes in a page or passage, and which kinds.
  • Fluency: reading smoothly without long pauses or sounding out each letter.
  • Retention: for memorisation, how much is still correct a week later.
  • Confidence: willingness to read aloud, including to others.
  • Consistency: lessons attended and practice sessions done.

Speed of finishing pages is the least useful measure. A student who finishes quickly with repeated mistakes has not progressed.

A simple tracking sheet

Keep a notebook or a spreadsheet with one row per lesson:

DateCoveredMain correctionPractice done (days)Notes
12 OctPage 4, lines 1-8Elongation in "qaala"4/5Hesitated on joined letters
14 OctPage 4, lines 9-15Same correction fixed5/5Smoother today

The "main correction" column is the most valuable. If the same correction appears three lessons in a row, the teacher should change the way it is being practised.

Milestones for reading beginners

Timelines vary a great deal with age, practice and starting point, so think in stages, not dates:

  • Knows all letters by sound and shape.
  • Reads short vowels accurately.
  • Reads sukoon, tanween and madd without prompting.
  • Joins letters into words smoothly.
  • Reads a short surah independently.
  • Reads a page of the Mushaf with few corrections.

The beginner roadmap describes these stages in more detail.

Milestones for Tajweed and Hifz

  • Tajweed: can name a rule, explain why it applies and apply it without reminder.
  • Hifz: recalls last week's material correctly without looking, and recent revision does not take longer than new learning.

The monthly review

Once a month, spend ten minutes with the teacher, or on your own for adult students:

  1. Look back at the log. What corrections have been fixed? Which keep returning?
  2. Ask the teacher what the student can do now that they could not last month.
  3. Pick one priority for next month.
  4. Adjust the schedule if lessons or practice are not working.

At Talqeen Academy, teachers give regular feedback on progress and a clear path after each stage, but families who keep their own log make that conversation much better.

Recordings as evidence

Record the student reading the same passage at the start and end of the month, with the teacher's agreement. Listening to both is often the most convincing proof of progress, especially for children.

When progress seems stuck

  • Check consistency first. Missed practice is the most common cause.
  • Ask whether the material is too hard. Sometimes a step back unlocks forward movement.
  • Look for a single recurring mistake. Fixing one habit can unblock everything.
  • Talk to the teacher about changing the approach.
