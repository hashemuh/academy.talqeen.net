Online Quran Classes for New Muslims: A Gentle Roadmap

Talqeen Academy · Updated 3 October 2026

If you have recently embraced Islam, learning to read the Quran can feel both exciting and intimidating. You may be starting from zero in Arabic, balancing work and family, and wondering where to begin.

This roadmap is built around real priorities: what you need for prayer first, then reading, then understanding. Every step can be taken at your own pace.

In this article

  1. Begin with what you need for prayer
  2. Use transliteration as a bridge, not a destination
  3. Learn the Arabic letters with a teacher
  4. Move on to reading short surahs
  5. Add meaning gradually
  6. Look after yourself on the journey
  7. Frequently asked questions

Begin with what you need for prayer

Most new Muslims want to pray correctly as soon as possible. A teacher can help you learn Al-Fatiha and a few short surahs by ear, with correct pronunciation, even before you can read the Arabic script fluently. This gives immediate benefit and builds confidence.

Use transliteration as a bridge, not a destination

Writing Arabic sounds in English letters can help in the first days, but it cannot capture many Arabic sounds. Treat it as temporary support while you learn the letters. A good teacher will gradually move you off it, usually within the first stages of reading.

Learn the Arabic letters with a teacher

Starting the alphabet with a live teacher means every sound is checked. The Noor Al-Bayan course teaches all 28 letters with fatha first, which keeps early lessons simple. Short, regular lessons are better than long, occasional ones. See the stages in the beginner roadmap.

Move on to reading short surahs

As your reading becomes steady, you begin reading surahs directly from the Mushaf. This is often a moment of real joy for new Muslims, as the script that was unreadable becomes meaningful.

Add meaning gradually

Understanding builds love for the Quran. Alongside reading practice, ask your teacher to explain the meaning of the short surahs you recite. Later, Quranic Arabic can deepen it.

Look after yourself on the journey

  • Expect slow days. Everyone has them.
  • Ask any question without embarrassment. Good teachers expect them.
  • Do not compare yourself with people who grew up reading Arabic.
  • Find community: family, local mosque, or fellow students.
