13 Online Quran Academies from the Source: A Comparison Checklist

Talqeen Academy · Updated 5 October 2026, 08:35:26 (UTC+03:00)

Choosing online Quran tuition starts with the learner's goal: introductory reading, Tajweed, Hifz, Arabic or guided understanding. A suitable teacher, clear curriculum and workable arrangements matter more than an unsupported “best academy” label.

The source introduction said ten academies, its heading said twelve, and its list contained thirteen. All thirteen entries are retained below. This is a rewritten account of the source's promotional descriptions, not a verified current ranking or recommendation to purchase a particular course.

How to Read the Comparisons

Check teacher competence, the type of feedback, time-zone availability, lesson format and fees. Current services can change. Native-speaking status does not by itself establish Tajweed expertise, and technology does not make learning effortless.

1. Mishkah Academy — Source Provider

The original provider advertised Quran, Arabic and Islamic studies for different ages and levels, with scholar-designed courses and certified male and female tutors from Islamic universities. It also claimed an established reputation, thousands of families and complete satisfaction.

Those reputation and satisfaction statements were not supported by independent assessment in the article. Its name is retained for the historical comparison, with no link or booking route to that academy.

2. Firdaws Academy

The source described Arabic, Quran Tajweed, Ijazah and Islamic studies lessons for varied ages and levels. It presented qualified, dedicated teachers and support for learners whose first language is not Arabic.

Its description connected the academy's purpose with using technology to make those subjects accessible online. Verify the actual curriculum, teacher qualifications and scope of any Ijazah programme.

3. Quran Ayat Institute

The source described Arabic and Quran lessons for adults and children, assigned homework and tutors who communicate in Arabic and English. It also claimed flexible scheduling suited to students.

Ask how homework is reviewed and whether the instructor can explain corrections in the learner's language. “Convenient without hassle” is promotional wording rather than a guarantee.

4. Resala Academy

The source promoted Arabic and Quran learning for non-Arabic speakers worldwide, including speaking and Quran reading. It claimed Egyptian native-Arabic teachers, affordable fees and courses in Quran, Arabic and Islamic studies.

Its promises of fast, effective and world-class low-cost service need actual evidence and a clear price comparison. Check whether the course addresses conversation, recitation or both.

5. Quran Sheikh Academy

The description listed Quran reading, Noorani Qaida, memorization, Ijazah, Tajweed, Arabic and Islamic studies. It advertised male and female tutors and home lessons for children, adults and women.

The source linked that provision with safety and qualified instruction. A tutor's gender or a home setting alone does not establish all learning safeguards or expertise; review the real arrangements and teacher qualifications.

6. International Quran Academy

The original claimed operation since 2000, live Skype and Zoom classes, certified teachers and students in more than thirty countries. Its course list included memorization, Tajweed, recitation, tafsir and Arabic.

It also claimed thousands of students had completed education and received certificates. These dates, numbers and platform descriptions remain source claims to verify. A completion certificate is not automatically a scholarly accreditation or Ijazah.

7. Quran Host Academy

The source said scholars founded this provider in 2010 and that it employs native-Arab and non-Arab teachers. It described home lessons in Qaida, Tajweed reading, memorization, tafsir and Arabic for children and adults.

The advertised ability to study without disrupting a busy schedule depends on actual lesson times. Its “pioneer” and “leading academy” claims were not substantiated through a comparison.

8. Almuhammadi Academy

The source described individual online Quran, Arabic and Islamic studies lessons for children and adults, delivered by native-Arab male and female instructors.

It claimed experienced teachers and advanced technology with interactive methods. Ask what those methods involve and how progress is checked rather than treating “advanced” as a measurable teaching outcome.

9. Be In Quran Academy

The description focused on Quran, Arabic and Islamic sciences for non-Arabic speakers. It claimed establishment in 2014 and more than 3,000 students across places including the USA, UK, Canada and Australia.

It also advertised male and female tutors, learners aged five to seventy, and a progression from letter shapes and sounds to Tajweed reading. All numbers and age arrangements should be confirmed; age alone does not establish a suitable level or pace.

10. Quran Spirit Academy

The source described home instruction with native-Arab tutors, courses including recitation, memorization and Tajweed, and individual lessons with certified teachers.

It presented an aim of helping Muslims read and understand the Quran. Its promise of beautiful recitation without any mistakes is not a result that can be guaranteed for every student.

11. Studio Arabiya Institute

The source discussed Arabic study in Egypt for visiting students and expatriates, alongside online Arabic, Quran and Islamic-related subjects. It claimed operation since 2011 and thousands of previous learners.

Online provision and in-person study abroad have different practical requirements. Verify actual services and relevant arrangements before relying on “highest-quality” or “world-class” descriptions.

12. Tarteel E Quran Institute

The description emphasised affordable home Quran lessons for children and adults, interactive online study, Tajweed, tarteel and personalised guidance by trained teachers.

It associated understanding and devotion with joy in learning. That is an educational aspiration, not proof of a uniform emotional experience or independently established teaching effectiveness.

13. Iqra Network

The source described courses adapted to students' goals and starting knowledge, covering children, adults and native or non-native Arabic speakers. It claimed accommodation of different budgets and instructors selected through interviews and further training.

Its mission statement aimed to make Islamic knowledge accessible, enjoyable and memorable while connecting texts with practical application. Verify how a particular course implements those aims instead of accepting the source's criticism of unspecified competing resources.

Choose through Evidence

The conclusion referred vaguely to “Quran Website” and “Quran Academy” without identifying one provider. It praised individual lessons, flexible scheduling, a clear interface, booking, lesson plans and progress tracking. Use those as features to investigate, not as evidence that an unnamed website is best.

Consult a teacher-selection guide and actual course information. Observe an available trial, ask how corrections are handled and choose a course that matches the learner's assessed needs.
