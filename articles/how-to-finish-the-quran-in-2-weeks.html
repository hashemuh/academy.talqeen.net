How to Finish Reading the Quran in Two Weeks: A Practical Plan

Talqeen Academy · Updated 3 October 2026, 18:23:18 (UTC+03:00)

Some readers aim to complete a Quran reading during Ramadan, around a special occasion, or as a personal study goal. A two-week plan needs enough daily reading time and a clear way to track the portions completed.

The source recommends a customized schedule, quieter periods such as Fajr and Asr, fewer distractions, aloud recitation, and a consistent Mushaf. Tracking tools may help you keep your place. The timetable should serve careful reading rather than pressure you to rush or skip pronunciation.

Eight Tips for a Two-Week Reading Plan

These tips preserve the source's eight-part outline while correcting the arithmetic and reference issues that affect the plan.

1. Create a Schedule That Prioritizes Quran Reading

Choose fixed study periods after prayers or during other dependable free time. Use a daily Quran reading schedule as a starting point, then adapt it to your actual pace.

The source suggests considering:

  • The total time available each day.
  • Quiet periods with fewer interruptions.
  • Breaks that allow you to return with attention.
  • Temporary changes to work or study arrangements where possible.

Protect the reading periods by reducing non-essential activities, while keeping the plan realistic. Test how long a portion takes you before committing to the full target.

2. Set an Accurate Daily Target

The source suggests 2–3 juz per day. For a 14-day goal, two juz every day are not enough: 2 × 14 = 28. You need to include additional portions across the schedule.

It also suggests 400 or more ayahs daily for a 15-day plan. At exactly 400, the result is 6,000 ayahs; the “or more” needs a defined target, so that figure alone is not a dependable completion plan. Ayahs also vary greatly in length.

Another source example is 100 ayahs after each of the five prayers, totaling 500 daily. That may help organize a count-based goal, but the reading time will vary with the passage. Juz boundaries or your Mushaf's actual pages usually make the completion point easier to track.

3. Consider Fajr and Asr for Longer Sessions

Morning and afternoon periods may offer quieter reading time for some learners. The source recommends assigning a larger portion of the day's work around Fajr and Asr, where those times fit your routine.

The original article attaches the wording “Establish prayer at the decline of the sun… and at dawn” to Surah Hud, 11:114. That reference needs correction: the wording about the sun's decline and dawn relates to Al-Isra, 17:78. Hud 11:114 refers to prayer at the two ends of the day and parts of the night. Neither verse is a specific two-week reading timetable.

Use prayer-related periods as practical anchors without presenting them as a requirement that every reader use the same schedule.

4. Plan for More Than Two Juz on Some Days

The Quran is divided into 30 juz. Two per day take 15 days, while three per day complete the 30-juz count in 10 days. For exactly two weeks, a mixed plan works.

5. Use a Suitable Progress Tracker

A simple record helps you know which portion remains. The source names Quran Companion, Tanzil, and Quran.com as tools or resources to consider and describes features such as goals, reminders, verse marking, and audio.

Those functions vary by product, version, and access level, so check what the tool you choose actually supports. You can also use a paper checklist. Keep one dependable record throughout the plan instead of losing track between several apps or copies.

6. Prepare a Quiet Reading Space

Choose a place with fewer interruptions, such as a quiet corner at home or an appropriate space in a mosque. Reduce phone notifications and other distractions, and explain the reading period to family members.

The aim is attentive study rather than simply remaining in the room for a set time. A manageable session with clear reading boundaries can be more useful than a long distracted one.

7. Recite Aloud at a Comfortable Level

The source recommends aloud recitation to keep attention on the words. Hearing your own reading can help you notice hesitation and practice pronunciation.

Aloud reading does not automatically correct Tajweed. Use a measured pace and ask a teacher about recurring difficulties. It can also reinforce familiar passages, while a completion plan is a reading goal rather than full Hifz. See practical fluency tips for related skills.

8. Keep a Consistent Mushaf

The source recommends a 16-line Mushaf for intensive reading and emphasizes keeping the same copy throughout the plan. A familiar layout can help you follow the passage and record your place.

A 16-line format is an option rather than a universal best choice. Use readable text and check the page count of the actual edition. Sticky notes or a suitable bookmark can show completed sections, but neither a particular layout nor a tracking method guarantees completion.

An Alternative Plan for 20 Days

The source suggests 1.5 juz daily for a 20-day plan. That calculation is exact: 1.5 × 20 = 30 juz. You can divide the daily amount around the five prayers instead of reading it in one sitting.

It also gives a page-based example of 30 pages daily: six after each prayer, or three before and three after each prayer. That totals 600 pages in 20 days. It applies only when your planned text has that page count; a 604-page Mushaf, matching the pagination shown for Surah An-Nas on Quran.com, needs four additional pages.

For a 604-page edition, one numerical option is 30 pages on 16 days and 31 on four days: 480 + 124 = 604. Check the actual beginning and ending pages and choose appropriate stopping points. Page-based targets should not be copied unchanged into a different Mushaf edition.

Let the Schedule Support Careful Reading

A completion goal needs a realistic pace, accurate boundaries, and a record of what you have read. Choose 14, 15, or 20 days according to the time you can give the task without abandoning careful recitation.

The source recommends Mishkah Academy's recitation classes and experienced tutors for tailored reading support. These are provider-specific promotional claims. When choosing your own lessons, ask the teacher to assess your reading and help set an appropriate amount.

Keep the purpose connected with reflection and a meaningful Quran routine. Completing a count is one goal; accuracy, understanding, and continued learning remain important.
