Why Tajweed Matters

Tajweed is not optional — it is how the Quran has been transmitted since it was revealed. Even a small mispronunciation can change the meaning of a verse. Our teachers ensure every rule is applied correctly from the start.

Makharij — Articulation Points

Where exactly in the mouth, throat, or lips each letter is produced. The foundation of correct recitation.

Sifaat — Letter Characteristics

The distinct qualities of each letter — heavy, light, nasal, whistling, and more.

Madd — Elongation Rules

When and how long to stretch vowel sounds — 2, 4, or 6 counts — depending on what follows.

Ghunnah & Noon Rules

The nasal sound of noon and meem, idghaam, iqlab, ikhfa, and izhar — with practical examples from the Quran.

Waqf & Ibtida

Where to stop and where to start when reciting — critical for prayer and public recitation.

Ijazah Preparation

For advanced students who want to receive an Ijazah in recitation — a formal chain of transmission back to the Prophet ﷺ.

Syllabus and Learning Stages

This sequence is adapted to the assessed starting level; familiar material can be reviewed and difficult skills revisited.

1

Reading assessment and articulation

Identify recurring errors and practise relevant makharij and letter characteristics through spoken models.

2

Noon, meem and ghunnah

Study applicable rules through carefully chosen words, listening, imitation and connected recitation.

3

Madd and connected reading

Practise vowel lengths and common recitation patterns according to the reading tradition being taught.

4

Stopping and consolidation

Apply waqf and ibtida to selected passages, revise earlier rules and read with decreasing reminders.

What Happens in a Lesson

  1. Recite a passage so the teacher can hear current strengths and errors.
  2. Study one relevant rule through a concise explanation and demonstration.
  3. Practise contrasting examples to hear and produce the difference.
  4. Apply the rule in connected Quran reading.
  5. Record the main correction and a short revision passage.

Assessment and Feedback

Assessment combines hearing your recitation with checking that you can explain and apply the relevant rule. Earlier corrections are revisited in new passages; memorising terminology alone is not treated as practical mastery.

Materials and Setup

  • A clear Mushaf used consistently with the teacher.
  • A notebook for examples and recurring recitation corrections.
  • Teacher-selected rule examples and suitable recitation recordings.
  • A reliable microphone so the teacher can hear articulation clearly.

Confirm the required edition and any material costs with the team before purchasing resources.

Realistic Progression

Progress depends on reading fluency, the corrections required and regular guided practice. Advanced recitation or Ijazah preparation has separate entry and assessment requirements; a beginner Tajweed course does not automatically award an Ijazah.

Possible next step: A Hifz plan with continuing recitation correction. The teacher can help you choose when the foundations are ready.
